# Data protection

Your venue keeps personal data about its staff and some of its customers: names, telephone
numbers, tax numbers, working hours. Data-protection law says what you must tell these people,
what you must keep on record, and what to do if the data is lost or exposed. This page explains
how the work is split between you and Waitron, and gives you templates to adapt with your adviser.
It is not legal advice, so ask your adviser to check each text before you use it.

## What the venue does and what Waitron does {#who-does-what}

In the law's words, your venue is the **controller**: it decides why and how personal data is
used. So the duties are yours. Waitron is the program you use to carry some of them out. Your
duties include these:

- Tell people about their data when you collect it. The sections below give you short notices for
  staff, invoice customers and guests who book, and a checklist for your full notice.
- Answer a person who asks to see, correct or delete their data, or to restrict or object to its
  use, or to receive it to take elsewhere, without undue delay and within one month of receiving the
  request. If a request is complex, or you have many, the law lets you take two more months where
  needed, as long as you tell the person, with your reasons, within the first month. Waitron keeps
  no list of these requests, so note each request, its date and your answer in your own records.
- Keep a written record of how you use personal data.
- Act if personal data is lost or exposed, and decide with your adviser whether to tell the AEPD,
  Spain's data-protection authority, and the people affected.
- Check the terms of the providers you chose. Your email provider and the provider of your backup
  storage handle data for you, so ask your adviser what contract you need with each. Card payment
  providers may act for you or answer for themselves, so check each one's terms.

Waitron helps with requests from people in these ways:

- Each person who signs in to the dashboard can download a copy of their own data, under
  **Account settings**, **Your details**, **Copy of your data**. See
  [Download your data](staff.md#own-data).
- An admin can download another person's copy from **Users**, with **Download their data** in
  the person's row menu. See [Download another person's data](staff.md#person-data).
- An admin can find an invoice customer's full invoices by tax number on **Orders** and download
  them. See [Download an invoice customer's data](staff.md#invoice-customer-data).
- A manager can correct a person's names and telephone with **Edit** on **Users**, and an admin
  can also change their email, role and status. See
  [Names and contact details](staff.md#staff-details).
- When an admin disables a person's account, Waitron deletes the person's PIN, password, passkeys,
  two-factor settings, recovery codes and Google link. See
  [Account access](staff.md#account-access).

The templates on this page are in English. The Spanish page, at `/help/es/data-protection`, holds
the same templates in Spanish, for the text you publish in Spain.

## A privacy notice for staff {#staff-notice}

The law lets you give people a short notice when you collect their data, as long as it names who
is responsible, why you use the data and that the person has rights, and tells them where to read
the rest. Give this one to each member of staff yourself when you take their details, for example
with their contract, on paper or in your own email. Replace each part in italics with your own
details, and cut anything that does not apply, such as the working-time record if you keep it
outside Waitron.

Copy and adapt the next paragraph:

**Data protection.** Controller: _your venue's legal name_ (_your tax number_), _your address_,
_your privacy email address_. We use your data to manage your contract, your access to Waitron and
the daily working-time record, which the law requires us to keep for four years. Waitron records
which member of staff rang up a sale, authorised a void, asked for or authorised a refund, and
opened the cash drawer or authorised its opening. You can exercise your rights of access,
rectification, erasure, restriction, objection and portability by writing to _your privacy email
address_, and you can complain to the AEPD. More information: _the address of your privacy page_.

The last line points to your full notice, which goes on your own web page. See
[The full notice on your web page](data-protection.md#full-notice).

Waitron shows your privacy page, the one you set on the **Privacy** card (see
[Your privacy page and email in Waitron](data-protection.md#privacy-settings)), in the emails it
sends staff to set up an account, reset a password or confirm a new email address, on the
dashboard's sign-in page and the pages an email's link opens to set up an account or reset a
password, in **Account settings** and in the `README.txt` of a data download. It also shows it,
with the privacy email, in the email that sends a full invoice (see
[A privacy notice for invoice customers](data-protection.md#invoice-notice)). Waitron holds one
page, so use the page of your full notice, which covers staff and customers alike.

## A privacy notice for invoice customers {#invoice-notice}

A customer who asks for a full invoice gives you their name, tax number and address. When the
customer is a person, including a sole trader, those details are personal data, and the notice is
due when staff take them. When the customer is a company, its own details are not personal data.

Copy and adapt the next paragraph:

**Data protection.** _Your venue's legal name_ uses your name, tax number and address to issue
this invoice and meet its tax duties, including sending the Tax Agency a record of it with your
name and tax number. Your rights and more
information: _your privacy email address_, _the address of your privacy page_.

When Waitron emails a full invoice, the email carries this notice, with your venue's legal name
and the privacy email and page from the **Privacy** card. The notice is in Spanish unless the
invoice is in English, and has the part about the Tax Agency only for an invoice the box sends a
record of. With neither the privacy email nor the page set, the email carries no notice. The email reaches the customer after staff have taken the
details, so show the notice where they take them too, for example on a sign beside the till.

If you also want it on paper, you can type a short version into the receipt's **Footer message**. In
**Departments and zones**, open the department and choose its **Receipts** tab. What you type there
prints at the bottom of the receipts the till prints for that department, simplified receipts and
full invoices alike. It also appears on the A4 copy of a full invoice, printed or as a PDF, the PDF
you email included. Three limits apply. You set it for each department separately. You write it once
for each language the receipt editor shows. It holds at most 200 characters, and the notice above is
longer than that, so use a short version such as this one, and count its length once your details
are in.

Copy and adapt the next paragraph:

**Data protection.** _Your venue's legal name_ uses your name, tax number and address to issue
your invoice. Rights and more: _the address of your privacy page_.

Whether the law asks for a privacy line on the invoice itself is a question for your adviser.

## What to tell a guest who books {#booking-notice}

A booking in Waitron holds the guest's name, an optional telephone number and notes, and no email
address, so Waitron cannot write to the guest. Staff take the booking, so the short notice is what
they say when they take it, by phone or in person.

Copy and adapt the next paragraph:

**Booking.** _Your venue's legal name_ keeps your name and phone number for the booking. If you
tell us about an allergy, we note it so we can prepare your food safely. You can ask us to see,
correct or delete your details, or use your other data protection rights, and there is more
information at _the address of your privacy page_.

## The full notice on your web page {#full-notice}

The full notice is the one each short notice points to. Put it on your own web page and use that
page's address in the short notices. Enter the same address on the **Privacy** card, so that
Waitron links to it (see
[Your privacy page and email in Waitron](data-protection.md#privacy-settings)). Cover each group of
people you hold data about: staff, invoice customers and guests who book. For each group, the law
asks the notice to say:

- who you are, and how to contact you; if you have a representative, who they are and how to
  contact them;
- how to contact your data protection officer, if you have one;
- why you use the data, and the legal basis for each use;
- where a use rests on legitimate interests, the interests you or someone else pursue;
- who receives the data, or which kinds of recipient, if anyone does, such as the Tax Agency for
  invoices, your email provider and the provider of your backup storage;
- where it applies, that you intend to transfer the data to a third country or an international
  organisation, as the law calls them, and whether the European Commission has found that
  country's protection adequate or which safeguards apply and how to get a copy of them; this can
  happen through the providers you chose, so ask each one where it keeps data, and ask your adviser
  which countries count;
- how long you keep the data, or, if you cannot say, how you decide; the law sets four years for
  the working-time record, and your adviser helps you set the rest;
- that the person may ask you for access to their data, and for its rectification or erasure, may
  ask you to restrict its use or object to it, and may ask for it in a portable form;
- where a use rests on consent, that the person can withdraw it at any time;
- that the person can complain to a supervisory authority, which in Spain is the AEPD, at
  https://www.aepd.es;
- whether the person has to give the data, by law, by contract or to enter into a contract, and what
  happens if they do not;
- whether you make automated decisions about the person, including profiling, and if you do, how
  they are made and what they mean for the person.

Copy and adapt the next paragraph, to open the page:

**Who is responsible for your data.** _Your venue's legal name_ (_your tax number_),
_your address_. Write to _your privacy email address_ about your data.

## Your privacy page and email in Waitron {#privacy-settings}

Waitron keeps two details of your privacy notice with the venue's other details: the address of
the web page that holds your full notice, and the email address people write to about their data.
To set them, open **Venue settings**, stay on the **Venue details** tab and find the **Privacy**
card. Choose **Edit**, fill in either or both, and choose **Save**.

- **Privacy notice web page** takes the page's full address, starting with `https://` or
  `http://`, such as `https://example.com/privacy`. Waitron refuses an address without that start,
  with a space inside it, or with a user name or password in it.
- **Privacy email** takes one email address, such as `privacy@example.com`.

To remove one, empty its field and choose **Save**. A manager or an admin can change them, and a
supervisor can see them. A configuration export carries them, so a new box set up from one shows
them. If you set it up with **New tax ID**, check them here: they are still the old business's until
you change them.

Waitron shows the page in the emails it sends staff to set up an account, reset a password or
confirm a new email address, on the dashboard's sign-in page and the pages an email's link opens to
set up an account or reset a password, in **Account settings** and in the `README.txt` of a data
download. A change shows in the next email or download; a page already open shows it once reloaded,
except **Account settings**, which follows it at once. The email that sends a full invoice also shows
whichever of the two are set.

## Your record of processing {#record}

The law asks a controller to keep a written record of how it uses personal data, and to show it to
the AEPD when the AEPD asks for it. An electronic document counts as written. Organisations
employing fewer than 250 people are spared, but not when their processing is likely to put people's
rights at risk, is not occasional, or includes special categories of data, such as health data. A
restaurant uses its staff's and its invoice customers' data every day, which is hard to call
occasional, and the law lists the three cases as alternatives, so any one of them is enough for the
exemption not to apply. Allergy notes can be health data as well. Ask your adviser to confirm that
you need a record, and whether your allergy notes are health data.

The law asks the record to say:

- your name and contact details, and those of any joint controller, representative or data
  protection officer you have;
- why you use the data;
- whose data it is, and what kinds of data;
- which kinds of recipient receive it, including any in a third country or an international
  organisation;
- where it applies, transfers of the data to a third country or an international organisation,
  naming which one, and, for one rare kind of transfer the law names, the documents showing the
  safeguards, which your adviser can tell you about;
- where possible, when you expect to delete each kind of data;
- where possible, a general description of how you keep it secure.

The entries below cover the main uses Waitron makes of personal data, one entry for each. Copy them
into your own record, replace each part in italics, and add what you do outside Waitron, such as
payroll run by your accountant. Two facts apply to every entry, so they are not repeated in each
one:

- Waitron does not encrypt the database itself, neither on the box's disk nor in the continuous copy
  in your backup bucket, so whoever gets hold of either can read every entry below. Scheduled
  backups are encrypted with the recovery key, which the box keeps.
- The copy in your bucket holds the whole database, so your backup storage provider receives
  everything below. Whether any data goes to a third country depends on the providers you chose, and
  Waitron does not check where they keep data, so check with each provider.

Copy and adapt the next paragraph to open the record, then each entry after it, up to the next
heading:

**Record of processing.** Controller: _your venue's legal name_ (_your tax number_), _your
address_, _your privacy email address_.

**Staff accounts and sign-in.** Waitron keeps an account for each person who uses the till or the
dashboard.

- Purpose: let staff sign in, and control what each person may do.
- Whose data: staff, including former staff.
- What data: names, email address, telephone, role, PIN, password, passkeys, two-factor settings, a
  Google link for a person who signs in with Google, and when and on which device each person
  signed in.
- Who receives it: your email provider, which delivers invitations and password-reset emails;
  Google, if you let staff sign in with Google; and _anyone else you share it with_.
- How long: _set with your adviser_.
- Security: Waitron stores PINs and passwords only as salted hashes, a deliberately slow scrambled
  form, so the database never holds them as typed. A short PIN can still be guessed from its hash
  with enough tries. Waitron encrypts the two-factor secret and stores sign-in tokens only as
  hashes. Disabling an account deletes the person's PIN, password, passkeys, two-factor settings,
  recovery codes and Google link.

**Employment, planning and absences.** If you plan staff in Waitron, it keeps each person's
contract details, rota and leave.

- Purpose: manage contracts, plan the rota and record leave.
- Whose data: staff, including former staff.
- What data: contract type, weekly hours, start and end dates, pay rate, shifts, availability,
  shift swaps, and absences with their kind and an optional note. An absence marked as sick leave
  can be health data, so ask your adviser how to treat it.
- Who receives it: _anyone you share it with, such as your payroll adviser_.
- How long: _set with your adviser_.
- Security: as for every entry.

**The working-time record.** If staff clock in and out in Waitron, it keeps the daily record of
working hours the law requires.

- Purpose: keep the working-time record the law requires.
- Whose data: staff, including former staff.
- What data: the times each person clocked in and out and started and ended each break, the device
  that recorded an entry where one did, who recorded each entry, each correction with its reason and
  who asked for, approved, declined or disputed it, and the email address each reminder went to.
- Who receives it: your email provider, which delivers the reminder emails Waitron sends a person
  who missed a clock-in or clock-out. The law says the record stays available to the workers, their
  legal representatives and the Labour Inspectorate.
- How long: four years, which the law sets.
- Security: Waitron refuses to change or delete an entry once it is written. Each person's copy of
  their own data includes their working-time record.

**Who did what at the till.** Waitron records who rang up sales, who authorised voids, who asked for
or authorised refunds, and who opened the cash drawer or authorised it.

- Purpose: know who made each sale, void, refund and cash-drawer opening.
- Whose data: staff.
- What data: who rang up a sale, who authorised a void, who asked for, authorised or recorded the
  outcome of a refund, who opened the cash drawer or authorised it, and when.
- Who receives it: _anyone you share it with_.
- How long: _set with your adviser_. Waitron never changes or deletes a recorded sale.
- Security: as for every entry.

**Full invoices, and emailing them.** A customer who asks for a full invoice gives you their
details, and Waitron can email them the invoice.

- Purpose: issue the invoice, send it, and meet the tax duties that come with it.
- Whose data: invoice customers who are people, including sole traders.
- What data: name, tax number, address and country, and the email address an invoice was sent to.
- Who receives it: the Tax Agency, which receives the customer's name and tax number, but not the
  address, with each invoice record; your email provider, for each invoice you email, which carries
  the invoice as a PDF with the customer's name, tax number and address.
- How long: _set with your adviser_. Waitron never changes or deletes an issued invoice. It also
  keeps every receipt and ticket it sends to a receipt or kitchen printer and deletes none of them,
  so the customer's details on an invoice printed at the till stay in the database too. An A4
  invoice is made again each time it is printed, and that printed copy is not kept.
- Security: as for every entry. Printed copies are yours to look after.

**Supplier invoices from sole traders.** When you enter a supplier's invoice in **Purchases**, the
supplier's name and tax number are personal data if the supplier is a sole trader.

- Purpose: keep supplier invoices for your VAT reporting.
- Whose data: suppliers who are sole traders.
- What data: the supplier's name and tax number, with the invoice's number, dates and amounts.
- Who receives it: _anyone you share it with, such as your tax adviser_.
- How long: _set with your adviser_.
- Security: as for every entry.

**Bookings.** Staff take bookings by phone or in person and enter them in Waitron.

- Purpose: hold a table for a guest.
- Whose data: guests who book.
- What data: name, telephone if given, notes, the date, time and party size, and which member of
  staff took the booking. A booking holds no email address.
- Who receives it: _anyone you share it with_.
- How long: _set with your adviser_.
- Security: as for every entry. Notes are free text, so ask staff to write only what service needs.

**Table names and notes.** Staff can give a table, or one of its bills, a name at the till, and type
notes on an order or a dish for the kitchen.

- Purpose: find the right table, and tell the kitchen what each order needs.
- Whose data: guests at a table, and anyone a note names.
- What data: the name typed for the table, which prints on kitchen tickets and on the table's
  receipts and invoices, the emailed PDF included; the name typed for a bill, which only the till
  shows; and the notes typed on orders and dishes, which print on kitchen tickets. A note is free
  text and can mention health, such as a diet, so ask your adviser how to treat it.
- Who receives it: your email provider, for each invoice you email; _anyone else you share it with_.
- How long: _set with your adviser_. Waitron keeps every receipt and ticket it sends to a receipt or
  kitchen printer and deletes none of them, so the names and notes printed on them stay in the
  database too.
- Security: as for every entry. Notes are free text, so ask staff to write only what service needs.

**Allergies.** Staff note a table's allergies so the kitchen can prepare food safely.

- Purpose: prepare each guest's food safely.
- Whose data: guests at a table.
- What data: a short description of who has the allergy, which allergens, and a note. Waitron keeps
  a copy with each kitchen ticket. This can be health data, so ask your adviser to confirm how to
  treat it.
- Who receives it: _anyone you share it with_.
- How long: _set with your adviser_. Waitron keeps every ticket it sends to a kitchen printer and
  deletes none of them, so the allergy details printed on kitchen tickets stay in the database too.
- Security: as for every entry.

**Card payments.** When a customer pays by card, Waitron keeps the payment's details.

- Purpose: take card payments and prove them.
- Whose data: customers who pay by card.
- What data: the card type, its last four digits, how the card was presented, the authorisation
  code and the payment provider's references.
- Who receives it: the card payment provider you chose. Its reader reads the card itself; Waitron
  sends the provider the amount and its own references for the order, and no customer details.
- How long: _set with your adviser_.
- Security: as for every entry.

**Backups and the configuration export.** Waitron copies the database continuously to a bucket you
own, can also make scheduled backups, and can export your setup to start another venue.

- Purpose: recover the venue after a failure, and set up a new venue from this one.
- Whose data: everyone in the entries above, in the bucket copy and in each scheduled backup; staff,
  including former staff, in the configuration export.
- What data: the bucket copy and each scheduled backup hold the whole database. The configuration
  export holds every person on the staff list with their name, email, telephone, role and status,
  and their employment records with pay rate and availability. It never holds a PIN, password,
  passkey, two-factor secret, recovery code or Google link.
- Who receives it: your backup storage provider, and the provider of any other bucket you send
  scheduled backups to; whoever you give a configuration export file to. Scheduled backups saved to
  **A folder on this box** stay wherever that folder is.
- How long: the bucket copy keeps a week of history. Each time Waitron stores a scheduled backup in
  a place, it deletes from that place any backup beyond the newest 7 and any older than 30 days,
  unless you change those settings. Before a backup, whenever the box's disk falls under about 2 GB free, or as the box starts with
  its disk full, the box can also delete its oldest backups in folders on that disk, never the newest in each. For a
  while after the box sets its own clock, it keeps some of the backups dated by the wrong clock or
  deletes them last; the start on a full disk does not do this yet
  ([Box status](box-status.md#free-space) explains). Turning off
  the bucket copy or the scheduled backups leaves what is already stored, so delete it at your
  provider, or from the folder, when you no longer need it. Anything else: _set with your adviser_.
- Security: Waitron does not encrypt the bucket copy, but the passwords and keys it keeps for your
  providers are encrypted inside it. Each scheduled backup is encrypted with the recovery key, which
  the box keeps. While it makes one, the box writes an unencrypted copy of the database on its own
  disk and deletes it when the backup ends, or at its next start if it stopped during a backup. The
  configuration export is encrypted under a passphrase of at least 12 characters, so it is as safe
  as that passphrase. A recovery pack made on **Backups** after you set up the bucket copy or
  scheduled backups opens them, so keep every recovery pack locked away, off the box.

## If personal data is lost or exposed {#breach}

When personal data is lost, stolen or seen by someone who should not see it, the law gives you
three duties. Start on them at once, because the second has a deadline.

First, write it down. You document every such incident, whether or not you tell anyone: the facts,
their effects and what you did about them. Waitron has no place for this record, so keep it with
your record of processing.

Second, the AEPD. You notify it without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours of
becoming aware of the incident, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to people's rights and
freedoms. A notification made after 72 hours gives the reasons for the delay. The notification
answers four questions:

- What happened? Where possible, which kinds of people and roughly how many, and which kinds of
  data and roughly how many records.
- Who can the AEPD contact? Your data protection officer if you have one, or another contact.
- What are the likely consequences for the people affected?
- What have you done, or what do you plan to do, to deal with it and to limit its effects?

You do not need every answer at once: the information may follow in stages, without further undue
delay. The AEPD takes notifications through an online form on its electronic office, the Sede
Electrónica. The AEPD's guide of June 2021 says that to open the form, the person who fills it in
needs a recognised electronic certificate or the Spanish government's Cl@ve permanente and PIN24H
sign-in systems, so check that
you have one before you need it. The form lets you send an initial notification before the 72 hours
are up, with what you know so far, and complete it within 30 days of that initial notification.

Third, the people affected. When the incident is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and
freedoms, you tell them without undue delay, in clear and plain language. One of the cases where
this is not needed is when the affected data was protected in a way that makes it unintelligible to
anyone not allowed to see it, such as encryption. Whether that holds when the key may have been
exposed too, as with a scheduled backup when the recovery key was lost as well, or a configuration
export whose passphrase may have leaked, is a question for your adviser. Waitron does not encrypt
the database copy in your bucket, so that copy gets no such protection from Waitron. The AEPD's
Comunica-Brecha RGPD tool helps you decide whether to tell the people affected.

Whether an incident must be notified to the AEPD, and whether the people affected must be told, is
your decision, made with your adviser. This page gives you the questions, not the answer.

## What Waitron can and cannot tell you {#breach-records}

To answer those questions you need facts: what was exposed, whose it was, and since when. This is
what the dashboard can show you.

- **Devices** shows each device's **Last seen**, the last time the box heard from it. A deleted
  device leaves that list, which takes its **Last seen** off the screen, so note it before you
  delete the device.
- No screen lists who signed in on which device. Each person's copy of their data does: an admin
  downloads it from **Users**, with **Download their data** in the person's row menu, and its
  `sign-in.json` file lists the person's sign-ins on each device, from when to when, and their
  dashboard sign-ins. See [Download another person's data](staff.md#person-data).
- On **Orders**, **View details** lists the receipt copies printed from **Orders** or from the
  till's **Transactions** tab, with who printed each. For a full invoice it also lists each time the
  invoice was printed or emailed, with who asked for it when someone did, and who downloaded its
  PDF.
- No screen shows who opened the cash drawer. Recent openings are among the jobs in **Print Queue**,
  on **Printers**, but no column says which jobs they are, and none names a person. Who opened the
  drawer, or authorised it, is in the `actions.json` file of that person's copy of their data.
- **Diagnostics** shows the box's recent log lines. Where Waitron writes a person's name, email or
  tax number as a detail it marks as personal, the line shows only a few of its characters.
  **Alerts** lists the alerts the box has raised.

Waitron does not record who opened or read a record on a screen, or the network address a sign-in
came from. The one address it takes from a request's connection and keeps is that of a device whose
clock helped confirm or set the box's clock. A print agent also tells the box its own network address,
which **Printers** shows.

## Common losses {#breach-losses}

Each loss below says what it exposes and what to do in Waitron. Then answer the questions in
[If personal data is lost or exposed](data-protection.md#breach) with what you found.

**A stolen till device.** A till's browser keeps a few things on the device itself: an identifier
for the last person who signed in on it, the list of dishes to make now with any notes typed on
them, and unfinished refunds with the reason typed for each. Disabling or deleting the device clears
nothing on it. The till app deletes a person's unfinished refunds when that person signs out or the
till locks itself, but keeps the other two. Refunds left when the till was closed, reloaded or
refused by the box, as it is once disabled, stay on the device. Act in this order. On **Devices**,
choose **Disable** in the device's row at once: it ends every staff sign-in on the device and stops
it connecting. Next, note its **Last seen**, and find who used it from the copies of each person's
data. Then choose **Delete**, which stops the device ever proving who it is again, and which Waitron
refuses while a payment is in progress on it. Until you delete it, a disabled device can ask to come
back and appear under **Devices asking to join**. Never choose **Enable** for a device you lost.

**A stolen phone or laptop used for the dashboard.** Disabling a device ends only its till sign-ins;
a dashboard sign-in belongs to the person. So an admin opens **Users** and chooses **Reset login**
for that person. It signs them out everywhere, deletes their PIN, password, passkeys, two-factor
settings, recovery codes and Google link, and sends them a new invitation if email is set up.
Waitron refuses it for the venue's only active admin. Within a minute of an earlier invitation,
**Reset login** or **Enable and send invitation** for the same person, it changes nothing and says
only that the invitation was not sent, so choose it again after a minute. The browser also keeps the
person's email address if they chose **Remember my email on this device** when signing in. If the
phone or laptop was also used as a till, follow the steps for a stolen till device as well.

**A lost or stolen box.** Waitron does not encrypt the database on the box's disk, so treat
everything in it as exposed: staff details and sign-ins, invoice customers, bookings, allergy notes
and every sale. PINs and passwords are stored only as hashes, but a short PIN can be guessed from
its hash. The box uses the passwords and keys it keeps for your providers, such as your email
server's, without anyone typing a key, so treat those as exposed too and change them at each
provider, your bucket's access keys included. Among them are the certificate the box uses to send
invoice records to the Tax Agency and its passphrase, so ask your adviser how to replace it. The box
also keeps the recovery key, which opens your scheduled backups. The key that protects two-factor
secrets is on the box too, so choose **Reset login** for each person who uses two-factor sign-in.
Waitron refuses **Reset login** for the venue's only active admin, who instead opens **Account
settings**, chooses **Disable** beside **Authenticator app**, and sets it up again.

**A lost recovery pack.** The pack made during first setup holds only the server key, which a
recovery needs alongside access to a copy; to replace it, see the end of this section. A pack made
on **Backups** after you set up the bucket copy holds your bucket's access
key and secret and the recovery key (the pack calls it the backup key), so whoever has it can read the whole database copy in your bucket and unlock the passwords and keys
stored in it, including the key that protects two-factor secrets, so also choose **Reset login** for
each person who uses two-factor sign-in. Waitron refuses **Reset login** for the venue's only
active admin, who instead opens **Account settings**, chooses **Disable** beside **Authenticator
app**, and sets it up again. The recovery key also opens your scheduled backups,
wherever that person can reach them. Revoke the old access keys at your bucket provider, which
Waitron cannot do for you. Then, on **Backups**, choose **Change bucket** and enter the new keys. If
your scheduled backups go to **The bucket copy's bucket**, also choose **Change destination or
schedule**, choose **The bucket copy's bucket** again, and save, because they keep the keys they
were saved with. A new recovery key does not change the passwords and keys for your other providers,
so change those at each provider too. While scheduled backups are on, **Backups** also offers
**Change the recovery key**; afterwards choose **Make recovery pack** on **Backups** and keep the new
pack. Scheduled backups made before the change still open with the old key, and so does the sealed
part holding the passwords and keys in the bucket copy's history from before the change, which is
kept for a week. A pack can also hold the server key. To replace it, make a new pack with **Also make
a new server key** ticked: new backups use the new key, and older backups still need the old one.

**A leaked configuration export.** The file holds every person on your staff list, former staff
included, with their name, email, telephone, role and status, and their employment records with
pay rate and availability. It never holds a PIN, password, passkey, two-factor secret, recovery
code or Google link. It is
encrypted under the passphrase chosen when it was downloaded, which has at least 12 characters, so
how exposed the data is depends on how strong that passphrase was and whether it leaked too. No
screen shows who downloaded an export.

**An account someone else used.** On **Users**, an admin chooses **Disable** or **Reset login** for
the person. Both sign the person out everywhere and delete their PIN, password, two-factor settings,
Google link, passkeys and recovery codes. **Reset login** sends a new invitation if email is set
up, and **Disable**
keeps the person out until an admin chooses **Enable and send invitation**. Within a minute of an
earlier invitation, **Reset login** or **Enable and send invitation** for the same person, **Reset
login** changes nothing and says only that the invitation was not sent, so choose it again after a
minute. Then look at what was done under the account: download the person's copy of their data, and
check **Orders**. See [Account access](staff.md#account-access).

**An invoice emailed to the wrong address.** The email carries the invoice as a PDF, which shows
the customer's name, tax number and address and what they bought. On **Orders**, **View details**
lists when the invoice was sent by email and who asked for it, but not the address it went to, so
ask the person who sent it. You can send it to the right address from the same place.
