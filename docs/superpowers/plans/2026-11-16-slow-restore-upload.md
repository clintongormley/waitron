# Let slow backup uploads finish

**Goal:** A progressing setup backup upload finishes beyond five minutes; ordinary routes keep a 300-second receive deadline. Source: Lane K’s `SLOW-RESTORE-UPLOAD` queue item. Separate Sol-high draft checked by the driver; Claude reviewed the plan on 16 November (228 seconds). The driver accepted the smaller setup-only scope and the corrections below. Watcher clearance is pending; no owner design approval is recorded.

**Reproduced:** On main `a25201632a`, a real HTTP restore-preview request sent 1 KiB each second and received 408 at 300011.524 ms. Its partial staging was removed. This measures the product route and receiver behind Node’s default listener, not a full appliance restore. Logs and disposable source: `~/waitron-campaign-k/receipts/slow-restore-upload/main-real-time-{reproduction.log,probe.ts}`.

## Scope and implementation

Reuse `receiveSetupArchive` for the unchanged 4 GiB ceiling, space checks and partial-file cleanup; Hono remains the body consumer. Add no dependency, configuration surface or stored record. Leave emergency handover, media, configuration transfer, whole-file recovery buffers and separate landing/recovery listeners alone. These three uploads are mounted by normal setup in `boot.ts`, not by those separate listeners.

- Add `apps/server/src/request-receive-limits.ts`: `attachRequestReceiveLimits(server, limits?)`, with internal shortened-duration injection for tests. Wire it only after the setup branch’s `startListening` call in `boot.ts` (currently line 1896), before requests arrive. Setup always supplies TLS. Leave `startListening` itself and every trading/upgrade/landing/recovery listener unchanged; transport coverage uses HTTPS only.
- **Server-wide change:** raise native `requestTimeout` from 300000 to 43200000 ms (12 hours); retain `headersTimeout = 60000`. Native timeout supplies the finite upload ceiling. Ordinary setup requests get a non-refreshing 300-second budget counted conservatively from `secureConnection`, or the last response’s `finish` already observed when a later request arrives. Do not start a fresh 300 seconds after headers. This may shorten the budget by connection/keepalive idle time; tests must cover slow headers and a later keepalive request. Trading listeners retain Node’s original accounting.
- Only POST raw paths (the part of `req.url` before `?`) `/setup-api/restore/preview`, `/setup-api/recovery/stage` and `/setup-api/restore` get the longer allowance; query strings do not change selection; do not normalize authority-like or encoded forms with a URL constructor. Proposed progress means at least 64 KiB received each two-minute window, checked each second through public `request.socket.bytesRead`; smaller completed bodies are accepted. This rate floor is Claude’s additional recommendation and needs explicit watcher clearance. If Hono/backpressure stops transport reads, count the lack of received bytes as a stall. Do not add body data listeners, resume it or change socket inactivity/keepalive settings. Four GiB at a constant 1 Mbit/s takes 9.54 hours by arithmetic, leaving some room under 12 hours.
- Expiry writes a raw 408 with `Connection: close` only before response headers were sent, then flushes/closes the socket; otherwise it closes the socket. Do not send a competing Hono response. Keep protection until `req.complete` is true or the socket closes, including after an early refusal response. Do not depend on body `end`/`close` or attach consuming listeners; completed-body processing has no receive deadline.
