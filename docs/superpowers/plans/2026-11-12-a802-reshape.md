**Goal:** Replace slice 4's one-step cash correction (#2867, squash `8b6bb438b3`) with a correction
that is opened first, gets its money back in one or more refunds of any payment kind, and files its
corrective invoice once all the money is back, then let a card refund confirmed with nobody signed
in file on the approval given at opening.

**Architecture:** A new correction row (one unfinished per ticket) holds what was approved at
opening. Money goes back through the existing Refund a payment code, each refund linked to the
correction; cash keeps #2867's "one pot" spreading. The corrective is filed by one helper: inside
the cash refund's own transaction, or, for a card, after the refund is recorded in its own
transaction. A background sweep raises alerts and (from the second pull request) files the waiting
card cases automatically.

**Tech stack:** TypeScript, Hono routes in `apps/server`, Drizzle over `node:sqlite`, Vitest with
`useVenueDb` (a real database) and the real Veri\*Factu backend.

## Words used here

- A **ticket** is a paid **simplified invoice** (type F2), the invoice a guest gets.
- A **corrective invoice** (type **R5**) is a new negative invoice that takes value off a ticket; once
  filed with AEAT (the Spanish tax agency) it cannot be undone. **Filing** it means writing it into
  the record chain with `recordCorrection`.
- The **split** is, for each VAT rate, the price before VAT (**base**) and the VAT.
- A **correction** is the new row this plan adds: what was asked for and approved, and its state.
- A **leg** is one refund (a `tender_refunds` row) linked to a correction. A **cash leg** may be
  several refunds, one per cash payment, made by one request. A **connected card** is a reader
  Waitron talks to; a **hand-keyed card** is a separate terminal staff work by hand, then tick
  "I have returned this money" (`apps/till/src/i18n/strings.ts:124–127`).
- A **transaction** is one all-or-nothing database step.
- The **opening approval** is the person whose permission `sale.rectify` (to file corrective
  invoices) allowed the correction to be opened: the signed-in person, or a supervisor by PIN.
- The **background pass** is `recoverTenderRefunds` (`apps/server/src/tender-refund-recovery.ts:24`),
  run about once a minute on the primary box, which asks card companies about refunds still waiting.
- A **drawer job** is the queued command that opens the cash drawer.
- A **replay** is the same request arriving again (a double tap, or a retry after a lost answer): it
  gets the first answer and changes nothing.
- An **alert** is an incident row the dashboard shows under an area, to people holding a permission.
- The **protected fiscal core** is `computeHuella` (the fingerprint each fiscal record carries of
  itself and the one before), the chaining of records, invoice numbering, the never-changed
  `registros_facturacion` table, and the record builders `recordSale`, `recordCorrection` and
  `recordSubstitution` (lane L runbook rule H2). The **golden huella test**
  (`packages/fiscal-verifactu/src/write-path.e2e.test.ts`) pins known fingerprints.
- **Starting migration**: each migration set has one, rebuilt from the TypeScript schema with
  `pnpm --filter @waitron/db db:generate` (`packages/db/package.json:29`); before go-live a rebuild
  means every venue is reset.

## Review focus

The five conditions most likely to bite a person that no single task's happy path exercises, each
pinned by a test in the task named:

1. **A lost answer to a cash leg, retried.** The till resends the same request: it must get the first
   legs back, not pay out again (Task 4, "answers a repeated cash request with its first legs").
   Sent again as a NEW request after the money is all back: refused, the correction is filed
   (Task 4, "refuses a second cash request once the corrective is filed").
2. **A card leg fails after cash has gone back.** €15 cash back, the €10 card refund fails: the
   correction stays open with €15 back; Finish now files −€15.00 (Task 6, "files for the money
   back when a card leg failed").
3. **The ticket's own filing becomes uncorrectable between opening and the last leg** (AEAT refused
   it, or another box took over, `packages/fiscal-verifactu/src/correction-admission.ts:53`, `:57`):
   the cash last leg is refused and undone; Close keeping the refunds ends the correction (Task 6,
   "closes keeping the refunds when the ticket can no longer be corrected").
4. **The clock falls behind the latest fiscal record at the last leg.** Cash: refused and undone
   (Task 4, the R5-refusal case covers the shape with the chain refusal; the clock refusal is owned
   by `carryNewTenderRefund`, already tested). Card: the refund stays recorded, the correction stays
   open, nothing filed (Task 5, "records a hand-keyed card last leg and files nothing").
5. **A second till corrects the same ticket, or gives an ordinary refund on it, while a correction
   is unfinished:** refused, naming the open correction so the till can resume it (Task 2, "refuses
   a second correction while one is unfinished"; Task 5, "refuses an ordinary refund on a ticket
   with an unfinished correction").

## 3. Decisions this plan makes that the note and the owner's answers do not settle

Each is the default the plan builds; each can be overruled before Task 1.

1. **Order: reshape first, then the fiscal-core change**, for the three reasons in §2.
2. **No automatic filing until PR 2.** In PR 1 a card last leg (connected or hand-keyed) is recorded
   and files nothing, whoever is signed in; the correction stays open with all its money back until
   someone presses Record correction. Building a session-based automatic filing in PR 1 would be
   replaced by PR 2's opening approval within one PR.
3. **Two server actions cover the four ways out.** `/file` files the corrective for the money that
   has gone back: the till labels it **Finish now** when less than the full amount has gone back and
   **Record correction** when all of it has. `/close` ends a correction: with nothing back it is
   **Discard**; with money back it is **Close keeping the refunds**, allowed only when the filing
   check refuses with `sale.correction_not_admitted` or `sale.correction_history_unusable` (the
   cases where the R5 can never be filed). Fewer routes, fewer refusal codes, one rule each.
4. **"Money back, corrective waiting" is not a stored state.** It is an open correction whose
   completed legs add up to its amount with none pending; the refusal that keeps it there travels in
   the alert. Stored states: `open`, `filed`, `discarded`, `closed`.
5. **The leg link has no amount column.** A leg is wholly for its correction; a correction leg
   refuses a tip, so the link's amount would always equal the refund's applied amount. (The cancel
   link has one because one refund can pay several cancels.) It does carry the till's request id,
   which the cash leg needs to answer a replay.
6. **Ordinary refunds on a ticket with an unfinished correction are refused**
   (`invoice_correction.already_open`), as the design asks (`…-design.md:245–247`).
7. **Each leg authorizes `sale.refund` as any refund does; the correction actions authorize
   `sale.rectify`.** For the one-tap all-cash case the till sends the same PIN with Open and with the
   cash leg from one confirmation; the PIN is never stored. Today every role holding one holds the
   other (`packages/identity/src/permissions.ts:14–15`; pinned in `permissions.test.ts`).
8. **The opening approval is re-read when used (PR 2):** the approver must still be an active
   person whose role holds `sale.rectify`; otherwise the filing is refused and falls back to the
   alert and Record correction. Same shape as `authorizeManager` re-reading a suspended manager
   (`packages/identity/src/manager-login.ts:186–199`, `record-correction.management.test.ts:129`).
9. **Alerts go to the tax-filing area, for managers:** a new claim
   `{ prefix: "invoice_correction.", area: "fiscal", permission: "fiscal.view" }` beside core's
   `chain.` and `clock.` (`packages/db/src/alerts.ts:5–11`). The wording sends them to the till's
   Find bill, where Record correction needs `sale.rectify` (supervisors and managers).
10. **Open does not ask the card company whether it takes a partial refund.** A ticket paid by a card
    whose provider refuses partial refunds opens, and its leg is refused `tender.refund_unavailable`
    (`tender-refunds.ts:110–117`); staff then Discard or Finish now. Checking it at Open would need the
    provider seat in the opening path for a rare case.
11. **Every filing re-runs the filing checks** (the regime's admission, the history, the chain check,
    the clock), at whatever moment it happens; the checks at Open are a forecast, not a substitute.
12. **The hour is measured from the correction's earliest completed leg**, checked by the background
    pass, raised once per ticket while unacknowledged (`recordIncidentOnce` deduplicates by code and
    sale, `packages/core/src/incidents.ts:124–151`; one unfinished correction per ticket makes that
    one per correction).
13. **Cash legs get their own route** (`POST /api/invoice-corrections/:id/cash`): cash is one pot and
    names no payment, so it does not fit Refund a payment's per-payment request. Card legs go through
    Refund a payment with `invoiceCorrectionId`.

`requested_by`, `authorized_by` and `ended_by` are plain person ids, with the same one-line comment
`tender_refunds` carries (`packages/db/src/schema/tender-refunds.ts:36`). Classification:
`invoice_corrections` is `classify(…, "ledger", "a till's correction of a paid invoice; only its
state moves, under a trigger")`, as `tender_refunds` is (`classification.ts:98–102`);
`invoice_correction_refunds` is `appendOnly(…, "ledger", …)`. Triggers, modelled on
`tender_refunds_guard_update` (`core.sql:290–334`): `invoice_corrections_guard_update` refuses any
