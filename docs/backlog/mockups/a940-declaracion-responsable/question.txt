A940 — Waitron's declaración responsable: four questions, asked one at a time

Background in one paragraph: Spanish law says the maker of invoicing software must publish a
written statement (the "declaración responsable") for every version it releases, saying that
version meets the Veri*Factu rules. It is never filed with anyone. It must be readable inside the
software on every till and the dashboard, and outside it before anyone installs. Nothing exists
today. The design (docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-a940-declaracion-responsable-design.md) drafts
the text, makes one per release, and adds "About Waitron" to every terminal (pictures 1-4).

Found while researching it, fixed first in a fiscal pull request you land yourself: every invoice
record tells AEAT the software version is "0.0.0", and puts the restaurant's name and tax number in
the fields AEAT reserves for the software's producer. As far as the repository shows, nothing has
been filed with AEAT for real yet, so no wrong record exists there.

FYI, no answer needed: the @waitron/verifactu library, published on its own, gets its own
declaration per library release, as the repository's July analysis of AEAT's rules says.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question 1 of 4 — who is the producer on the first declaration?

The declaration gives the producer's name, Spanish tax number (NIF) and a full postal contact
address. It is shown on every box and on the public GitHub releases page. The same name and tax
number also go into every invoice record AEAT receives, in the "producer" fields.

Example: the deli goes live on version 1.0.0. Its declaration says «Nombre y apellidos de la persona
productora: <your name>», then your NIF, then the address you choose, and every record the deli
files carries your name and NIF as the software's producer. In 2027 a Waitron company exists;
release 1.4.0 names it, and from that release every new record carries the company's NIF instead —
part-way through the deli's running chain of records. AEAT's developer FAQ ties the producer's NIF
to the system's two-character code «como si formara parte de él», and says a change in the
system's identity makes it a different system. So the switch most likely means a new installation number and a new chain of records at
every venue running then — today that would be the deli alone. The asesor confirms the cost.

- A (recommended) — you personally, now: your name, your NIF, and a postal address you are content
  to publish (it need not be your home, but it must reach you). Reply with the three facts, or say
  you will put them in yourself. When the company comes, the deli starts a new chain (a path the
  box already has).
- B — form the company first (compliance plan Step 6) and wait to release until it exists, so no
  venue ever has to switch.

Question 2 of 4 — the two yes/no answers

The declaration must say (e) whether Waitron can only work as Veri*Factu, and (f) whether one
installation can keep the invoicing of several taxpayers. Each invoice record carries the same two
answers.

Example: a box holds exactly one company (the deli), and a Spanish box can only file the
Veri*Factu way. So (e) = Yes and (f) = No. Today the records say (e) Yes and (f) Yes — the (f) Yes
was a placeholder the code itself marks "unverified". One wrinkle for (e): the same build also has
a no-filing mode, which only a UK venue gets (and the setup wizard does not offer the UK yet).
Whether that counts as "can also work outside
Veri*Factu" is a legal reading.

- A (recommended) — (e) Yes, (f) No; the records change to (f) No in the same fiscal fix; ask the
  asesor to confirm both, including the UK mode, in the next batch.
- B — keep (f) Yes, in case one box ever serves two companies (a future version could say Yes
  when it can actually do that).

Question 3 of 4 — the wording

The item headings are AEAT's own worked example, word for word, with three small departures: the
title is the law's own wording, the annex says «persona productora» as the main items do, and the
annex leaves out the long "how we comply" item. The parts that are ours:

(a) name: «Waitron» (today the records say "Waitron POS"; they change to match)

(d) what it is — «Waitron es un software de punto de venta para restaurantes y bares. Se instala
en un equipo servidor del propio usuario, situado en su establecimiento, y los terminales del
establecimiento se conectan a él por la red local.» Then its parts: the server (makes, chains,
keeps and sends the invoice records), the @waitron/verifactu library with its version and its own
declaration (it builds each record, its fingerprint and the QR code's contents, and sends the
record), the till,
handheld, kitchen-screen and dashboard apps («Los terminales no generan registros de
facturación»), the print agent, and the outside parts we take responsibility for (Node.js with
SQLite, Litestream, Linux with Docker; the full list is on the box). Optional services of ours
this version uses: «ninguno». No hardware: the server is the venue's own machine.
«Cada instalación lleva la facturación de un único obligado tributario.»

(d) main functions, checked at each release against what the software can really do. On main
today: «toma de pedidos por mesa o en barra, cobro en efectivo y con tarjeta, expedición de
facturas simplificadas, facturas rectificativas, consulta de las facturas emitidas e informes de
ventas». Full invoices, substitute invoices, cancellations and recording the end-of-day till close
are left out because no screen can do them yet.

(g) no signature of our own: AEAT's example sentence, unchanged.

Annex: info@waitron.io, and the GitHub releases page as the archive of every version's declaration.

Example: a customer reads (d) and asks for a full invoice. Today the till refuses one, so (d) must
not promise it; the release that turns full invoices on adds them to the list.

Question 4 of 4 — production boxes run releases only

Every version needs its own declaration, however small the change. A box that installs whatever is
on main would be running a version with no declaration.

Example: Friday you push a fix to main and run `waitron.sh install` on the deli's box, which
installs main. On Saturday it is selling on a version nobody declared. With releases only, the
fix reaches the deli when you cut 1.0.1 (two commands, which write and freeze 1.0.1's declaration
in a pull request and then tag it).

- A (recommended) — a production box refuses to start on a build that is not a tagged release
  (the recovery page says "This box is running a test build. Install a release."), and
  `waitron.sh install v1.0.1` pulls the published image. Test boxes run anything. The check runs
  only at start, never during service.
- B — allow any build in production and rely on the habit of releasing.

Revision question 2 of 2 — how the automatic identity change names the new installation

A new installation needs a name no box of the same business has used. Today's rule (A679) uses the
current hour and first asks a person "has anyone set up a Waitron box for this business in the
last hour?", waiting until the next hour if the answer is yes or don't know. A box starting by
itself after an upgrade cannot answer that.

Example: the bar's box starts on 1.4.0 at 11:15 on 1 March 2027.
- With A, it takes the hour-and-minute name A947 proposes for a switch to the spare box: 09YA-15,
  series A-09YA-15, and normally sells at 11:15. Your second venue's main box, upgraded at 11:17,
  gets 09YA-17.
- With B, it uses the four-character hour and takes the "don't know" path: it sells nothing until
  12:00, then uses 09YB.
- With C, it waits until a manager opens the dashboard and answers the question.

- A (recommended) — the hour-and-minute name, with A947's check against every name the box knows.
  It normally sells again at once; if a name it knows is a little later than its clock it waits up
  to about three minutes, and if one is more than three minutes ahead it refuses until a minute it
  names, then tries again by itself at that minute (no invoices meanwhile). Limit: two of the same business's venues changing in the same
  minute would take the same name, so the release runbook says to upgrade one venue's main box at
  a time, a minute apart (each venue's spare in the same visit). Needs you to approve A947's
  revision (its slice 4a builds the name).
- B — the four-character hour after waiting for the next hour: no new name form, but up to an hour
  without invoices after such an upgrade, and the same limit within one hour.
- C — a manager answers on the dashboard; no invoices until one does.

Second revision, question 2 of 2 — A993 with no internet: when does the box change?

The problem: A947 refuses to take a name without a second clock. The change runs with nobody
present, so its only possible second clock is the internet's. At a one-box venue whose till is the
box's own screen, with no internet, the box would sell nothing until someone opened a page on a
phone — something outside blocking a sale, which the project's rules forbid.

Example for every option: the same bar, one box, its screen is the till. The box downloaded 1.4.0
the night before; the upgrade switches it on at 11:15 on 1 March 2027, and the fibre is down until
14:00. (With the internet up, all four options do the same: 0CNNW closes, 0JMV7 opens.)

- A (recommended) — the upgrade makes the change itself, before 1.4.0 serves, and only while it can
  read the internet's clock. With no internet it changes nothing, starts 1.3.2 again, and reports
  "upgrade pending: needs the internet". At 11:15 the bar goes on selling on 1.3.2, receipts
  A-0CNNW/1235 onwards, each naming you and version 1.3.2 — both true. Next morning at 09:05 the
  fibre is back, the upgrade runs again and takes 0JP45 (first receipt A-0JP45/1). Cost: a release
  that changes the producer can reach a box late; no sale waits. Releases only ever arrive by
  download today, so the internet is normally there at the moment of upgrading.
- B — 1.4.0 starts at once and sells on the old installation, under your name, until it can read
  the internet: A-0CNNW/1235 onwards on 1.4.0, then 0JN0C at 14:00. Cost: those receipts' records
  would say you produced version 1.4.0, which you never declared — on our reading the law's wording
  does not allow it, so the asesor would have to say yes first; and two of the design's checks
  would be loosened.
- C — 1.4.0 starts and waits for a till's or phone's clock. At this bar nothing is sold until the
  manager opens the box's page on her phone (the box's own screen does not count). Cost: a sale
  waits on a phone at every one-box venue.
- D — this one change trusts the box's own clock. It takes 0JMV7 at once. Cost: an exception to
  A947's rule; if the box's clock were two hours fast, another of your boxes could take the same
  name two hours later without knowing.
