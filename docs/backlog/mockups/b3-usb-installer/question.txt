B3 — THE BOOTABLE USB INSTALLER: REVISED FOR YOUR FIVE ANSWERS — TWO QUESTIONS, ONE AT A TIME
Design: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-b3-usb-installer-design.md (revision note at the top; §18).
Pictures in this folder. Ask 1, then 2. Each has a recommendation; the last option is always
"Stop here — I'll write".

In short: a small stick boots an installer that, with nobody at the box, fetches the newest release,
checks its signature, writes it onto the box's disk inside an encrypted container and restarts once
the stick is out. Your three guards are all in. The disk's key lives in the box's security chip; a
printed recovery key opens it when the chip will not. The box's screen runs the setup wizard, then
the dashboard, and can become a till; its settings are a Waitron page. A setup code stops a stranger
setting up a new box first.

QUESTION 1 of 2 — approve the revision?
Pictures: 1-unattended-install.png, 2-existing-box.png, 7-till-and-dashboard.png, 10-setup-code.png
(then 3, 6, 8, 9)

Example: Monday 10:00, the deli's new box arrives with no screen. Marta plugs in the cable and the
stick and presses the power button. At 10:01 the installer has copied itself into memory and
written the box's setup code onto the stick; from then on the stick may come out (the card gives the
time). It checks the box has its security chip and Secure Boot switched on. By 10:02 it has the
internet time and has fetched and checked Waitron 1.4.0; at 10:03 it erases the Windows the box came
with (60 seconds after the check — nobody sees it). She takes the stick out at 10:05 and the box
restarts itself at 10:07, seals its disk key into its chip and restarts once more to prove it. At
10:10 she opens waitron.local on her phone, types the setup code from the stick's
WAITRON/codigos-de-configuracion.txt (read on the shop laptop), and sets up the venue. The recovery
sheet the wizard prints now also carries the disk's recovery key. Friday: a power cut, and someone has put the stick back in. The
box starts from the stick, sees «Bar Santet», waits 15 seconds, and starts its own disk: one extra
restart, no erase.

The calls you might want to overturn (all mine, all in the design):
  - a stick in a Waitron box STARTS the box after 15 seconds rather than stopping (your guard 3,
    "refuse to do anything", without leaving the venue dark until someone finds the stick);
  - 60 seconds' wait before erasing another system, which a key can stop;
  - the installer's hotspot is open (no password), and passes nothing on; and once a box holds a
    venue it opens NO hotspot — a passer-by could otherwise move it onto their own Wi-Fi and cut the
    tills off; a set-up box changes Wi-Fi from Settings, or with a cable;
  - a disk the installer cannot recognise (a damaged Waitron disk, someone else's encrypted disk)
    is never erased without a keyboard;
  - the installer refuses a box with Secure Boot or the security chip switched off, before erasing;
  - NO hand-set clock: your answer listed "the clock" as a setting, but A798 (approved) says "No set
    the clock action anywhere in Waitron". Every install now sets the clock from the internet, so I
    kept A798. Say if you want it reopened;
  - «Panel» in the till's menu signs the till's person out first, and «Volver a la caja» signs the
    dashboard out, so a manager never comes back to the till as the waiter;
  - maintenance access for admins only, shown once, used only at the box's own screen (no SSH);
  - the setup code is made by the installer and written onto the stick before it says the stick can
    come out (and already filled in on the box's own screen); every setup request needs it, the
    box's naming page included.
Found on the way: Debian 13 cannot unlock its main disk from the chip out of the box (an open
Debian bug), so the box uses a different startup tool (dracut). The obvious chip setting would let
any Debian stick unlock the disk, and systemd's own protections need a kind of signed kernel this box
cannot use, so Waitron adds a small step of its own, and the first build must prove the result with
eight experiments, each with a control. One case stays open: the day Debian moves to Microsoft's
newer certificate, that update waits for a person with the recovery key until the first build
settles how to take it unattended.

  A (recommended) — approve as written. Plan slices 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 8 (the go-live minimum) next.
  B — approve with changes (say which).

QUESTION 2 of 2 — practising on a box installed from the stick
Picture: 11-q2-practise.png (and 10-setup-code.png, lower half)

Example: the deli gets its box on Monday and wants to practise with its real menu before it starts
filing with AEAT on the 1st.

  A (recommended) — a stick box offers only Live, restore or spare (like a production box). The deli
    practises with Prepare on another computer (installed by hand with waitron.sh), then brings its
    menus and layouts across at Live setup with the existing configuration copy. The box never holds
    a practice database. This also fixes W60's "production box offers Demo and Prepare, then fails".
  B — the stick box offers Prepare too, switching itself to pre-production. When the deli is ready it
    exports its configuration, reinstalls the box from the stick, and brings the menus across at Live
    setup. Practice happens on the real box and screen, at the cost of one reinstall before going live.
