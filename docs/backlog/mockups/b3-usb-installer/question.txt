B3 FYI: unlock reachability and normal Wi-Fi policy are settled

Your 26 October ~13:45 and ~14:00 CET answers are folded into design §7.5/§18 and the
six-slice plan. This replaces the earlier A/B/C question; no policy answer is outstanding.
Planning remains authorised; building still needs its own go-ahead. No setup code, new sign-in
or first-claim authority is added. Pictures 6, 9 and 12 now illustrate these decisions in both languages/themes and widths.

While the box is locked, http://waitron.local redirects straight to the trusted per-disk
https://unlock-<installation-id>.waitron.local address. HTTPS waitron.local instead uses a
deliberately untrusted certificate NOT signed by the box CA. You accepted clicking past that
one warning only to reach the redirect. Neither entry point has a key form. Enter the disk
key only locally or at the verified trusted recovery hostname; the sheet keeps its full URL,
disk UUID and fingerprints. Never bypass a warning on the actual key form.

The normal server certificate/private key and CA signing key stay encrypted. A separate
CA-signed recovery leaf private key and a separate untrusted redirect-only leaf key stay
outside encryption. Physical access exposes the recovery leaf key; it is not the CA signing
key and does not mint other certificates. The unsigned startup program still cannot prove
itself honest. The screen warns when readable startup checks differ or cannot be made; after
unlock the host checks its encrypted record before resealing.

A Wi-Fi box may keep the NORMAL Wi-Fi SSID/password and only necessary join fields in the
unencrypted boot partition. Someone holding the box can read that password, including by
taking out its disk or starting another signed Debian stick. You accepted that cost. The
operator guide must say: if a box is stolen, change the venue's Wi-Fi password.

The running host updates the minimal fixed-path copy whenever Wi-Fi settings change and
removes it for cable-only use. Design §7.5/plan 3.1 propose restricted durable/atomic writes,
obsolete-copy cleanup and interrupted-operation reconciliation without touching measured
boot bytes. No arbitrary paths, logs, disk keys, other credentials or credential-bearing URL
are copied. A stale/missing/corrupt copy, an unavailable network or a cut update may prevent
joining; the fallback is cable or local display/input, not a promise of working Wi-Fi.
There is no separate recovery SSID or unlock hotspot. The installer-only open hotspot stays.

The existing shared wrong-key backoff remains a plan proposal: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, then 60 seconds,
capped at 60 until success or reboot. Those numbers were not explicitly chosen in your two
answers. A passer-by's wrong keys can delay local and remote recovery; reboot clears the
memory-only delay, which does not limit offline attacks on a stolen disk.

one Android phone accepting a CA-signed outside-subtree leaf on 8 September, without captured

Node TLS receipts do not qualify Android. Failed negatives or an inconclusive positive block
