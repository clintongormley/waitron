SAME box, Prepare first, then fresh Live setup from the dashboard, without USB or OS reinstall.
Do not ask Q2 again. Its recorded answer is in the design's §18.

A small stick boots an installer that, with nobody at the box, fetches the newest release,
checks its signature, writes it onto the box's disk inside an encrypted container and restarts once
the stick is out. Your three guards are all in. The disk's key lives in the box's security chip; a
printed recovery key opens it when the chip will not. The box's screen runs the setup wizard, then
the dashboard, and can become a till; its settings are a Waitron page. A setup code stops a stranger
setting up a new box first. A stick box offers Prepare too; the admin's later action saves only
allowed configuration and clears the practice venue before restarting private first setup in Live.
The disk, encryption and installed system stay; no practice sale, invoice number or chain crosses.

Example (all times illustrative, not measured): Monday 10:00, the deli's new box arrives with no
screen. Marta plugs in the cable and the stick and presses the power button. At 10:01 the installer has copied itself into memory, checked
the box has its security chip and Secure Boot switched on and holds no Waitron, and written the
box's setup code onto the stick; from then on the stick may come out (the card gives the time). By
10:02 it has the
internet time and has fetched and checked Waitron 1.4.0; at 10:03 it erases the Windows the box came
with (60 seconds after the check — nobody sees it). She takes the stick out at 10:05 and the box
restarts itself at 10:07, seals its disk key into its chip and restarts once more to prove it. At
10:10 she opens waitron.local on her phone, types the setup code from the stick's
WAITRON/codigos-de-configuracion.txt (read on the shop laptop), and chooses Prepare on that SAME
box. She keeps the disk recovery sheet, prepares the real menus and floor plans, and the staff
practise there.

On opening day, Marta signs in as admin and proves who she is again with her password or passkey,
plus her enrolled authenticator code. In the dashboard she chooses «Empezar en Live con esta
configuración». She reviews the copy/reconnect list and keeps the new private recovery code before
confirming. The box validates and durably saves only the allowed configuration, records the action,
clears practice data and restarts first setup with Live selected and that configuration ready to
load. No USB, no OS reinstall, no disk/encryption reset. Her remote browser resumes privately; the
original code on the stick is exhausted. The managed box console also has a private way in; another
browser on the LAN cannot take over.

She creates the Live admin and credentials, checks the venue/taxpayer and receipt-language/series
defaults, supplies Live's fiscal certificate and completes the ordinary Live checks and recovery
steps. Staff records cross suspended, without their passwords, PINs, passkeys or TOTP enrolment;
she reviews and reactivates them with fresh credentials. Printer/print-agent definitions cross
inactive and need reconnecting and enabling. Device profiles cross, but the tills (including this
box's own till) must join again; card readers, mail, backups and Cloud connections need setting up
as needed. Practice sales, invoice numbers and chains do not cross. She keeps the original disk
recovery sheet, since the host cannot promise to reprint its already-deleted copy of that key.

Friday, after going Live: a power cut, and someone has put the stick back in. The box starts from
the stick, sees «Bar Santet», waits 15 seconds, and starts its own disk: one extra restart, no erase,
and no new code written to the stick.

The design details included in this approval:
  - a stick in a Waitron box STARTS the box after 15 seconds rather than stopping (your guard 3,
    "refuse to do anything", without leaving the venue dark until someone finds the stick);
  - 60 seconds' wait before erasing another system, which a key can stop;
  - the installer's hotspot is open (no password), and passes nothing on; and once a box holds a
    venue it opens NO hotspot — a passer-by could otherwise move it onto their own Wi-Fi and cut the
    tills off; a set-up box changes Wi-Fi from Settings, or with a cable;
  - a disk the installer cannot recognise (a damaged Waitron disk, someone else's encrypted disk)
    is never erased without a keyboard;
  - the installer refuses a box with Secure Boot or the security chip switched off, before erasing;
  - NO typed clock time: this concerns the time instant. You choose the time zone through the
    setup area (country-pack default), and edit it later in venue details under that screen's
    existing history limits. The original clock decision and its dated A798 update stay in §10;
    (2026-10-24: you reopened it for A798. The box is to set its own clock from AEAT's time, two
    agreeing tills or an administrator's own phone or computer, never a typed time: A798-B slice
    9b, not built yet. See the B3 design's §10.)
  - «Panel» in the till's menu signs the till's person out first, and «Volver a la caja» signs the
    dashboard out, so a manager never comes back to the till as the waiter;
  - maintenance access for admins only, shown once, used only at the box's own screen (no SSH);
  - the setup code is made by the installer and written onto the stick before it says the stick can
    come out (and already filled in on the box's own screen); every setup request needs it, the
    box's naming page included; Prepare completing setup exhausts that original code;
  - the same-box action is admin-only with fresh reauthentication. Its configuration, private new
    setup/resume credentials and audit survive outside the venue being removed. A missing module,
    invalid import or missing required language must refuse before removal. A989 remains OPEN
    and must close before this action can be built. Interrupted cutover resumes the same operation
    without erasing a newly created Live venue. Concrete reset/locking APIs are for the later plan
    after A1042's release. This is Prepare to fresh Live only, never a Live-box eraser.
Found on the way: Debian 13 cannot unlock its main disk from the chip out of the box (an open
Debian bug), so the box uses a different startup tool (dracut). The obvious chip setting would let
any Debian stick unlock the disk, and systemd's own protections need a kind of signed kernel this box
cannot use, so Waitron adds a small step of its own, and the first builds must prove the result with
nine experiments, with controls where a broken test would look like a pass. One case stays open: the day Debian moves to Microsoft's
newer certificate, that update waits for a person with the recovery key until the first build
settles how to take it unattended.
