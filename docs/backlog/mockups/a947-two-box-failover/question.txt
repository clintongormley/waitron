Example: Friday 23 October, Bar Santet. 21:39 the bar box's power supply trips. 21:40 Marta
taps «Cambiar» on the terrace till, ticks "unplugged", and signs in with her password and the
code on her phone (or her passkey) — no recovery sheet. The back-room box sells at 21:41 as
A-06XV-40/1: your hour code 06XV plus the minute, 40. 21:47 the bar box is plugged back in:
it hands over its three last-second sales, checks the back-room box has all three (same number,
same fingerprint, one by one) and is healthy, archives its own database, wipes itself and
becomes the spare — nobody asked. 21:55 the back-room box's disk fails; Marta switches again,
and the bar box sells at once as A-06XV-55/1. Under the hour rule alone it would have waited
until 22:00. AEAT sees three installation numbers (06RB, 06XV-40, 06XV-55), each with its own
chain, nothing repeated.

What changed, in short:
- The switch's series carry the hour AND the minute, on every switch (not only a second one in
  the hour) — so the switch never asks your setup question "has anyone set up a box in the
  last hour?". The two forms can never be equal. Only two switches in the SAME minute wait,
  for the next minute. Fiscal: its own pull request, which you land.
- The switch asks for the manager's passkey, or password plus authenticator code if they have
  one — the same on a till as on the dashboard. No installation code, no emergency key.
- Any device can reach the switch, even after a reload: the spare answers waitron.local with
  only its switch page while the main box is silent, and each box carries a QR label to its
  own switch page. A passkey works on both (tested in Chromium with a software passkey;
  real phones not yet). With no internet, a phone's
  passkey cannot be used at a till (it needs an internet relay); the password, a passkey on the
  device itself, or the manager's own phone scanning the label all work.
- "Do we need the recovery key?" Only to restore a box from a backup (and to re-register one).
  It does not help when nobody can sign in — that is the emailed reset or a reset on the box.
- The returning box becomes the spare by itself after three checks. A manager may override only
  the health check (e.g. no internet); never the "records arrived" check or the archive. The
  archive is kept 90 days.
- Found on the way: today both boxes give tills the same address, so a till sees one server,
  not two. The revision gives each box its own name.

- A (recommended) — approve. The build plan is written next; the fiscal slices (the switch's
  series, promotion, the hand-over, the return, the tail, becoming the spare) wait for you to
  land them.
- B — approve, but use the minute form only for a second switch in the same hour; the first
  switch of an hour keeps the plain four-symbol name and asks the "last hour" question.

A947 — go-live on two local boxes: four questions, asked one at a time.
Design: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-a947-two-box-failover-design.md (§12).

In short: the main box sells and streams everything it writes to the spare box, about a second
behind, with no internet needed. When the main box dies, a manager switches the venue to the spare
from any till; open tables are still there. When the old main box comes back it hands over the few
sales the spare never saw, and becomes the new spare. Nothing is filed twice and no invoice number
is reused: the spare is switched on under a fresh hour-coded series of its own, chosen at the
moment of the switch (your A679 answer of 2026-10-16), never the main box's.

Question 1 of 4 — who switches to the spare box?
Picture: 5-q1-who-switches.png (also 1-main-not-answering.png and 2-take-over-dialog.png)

Example: Friday 21:40, the main box's power supply fails in the middle of service.

- A (recommended) — a manager switches, from any till. After 20 seconds every till shows a red
  strip "El equipo principal no responde"; a manager taps «Cambiar al equipo de reserva», unplugs
  the main box, types the main box's installation code from its recovery sheet, and their password and
  authenticator code. About two minutes without selling (up to an hour if a second switch falls in
  the same clock hour, by your hour-code rule). If the main box is in fact still up (one till's cable is out), the
  dialog refuses and offers a hand-over that loses nothing.
- B — the boxes switch by themselves after about a minute. Faster when the main box really died.
  But two boxes cannot tell "the main box is dead" from "the main box is cut off", so a pulled
  cable or a failed switch between two rooms leaves both boxes selling, each to the tills it can
  reach: fiscally safe, but two sets of open tables. It also needs your W41s-4 answer changed,
  because that take-over asks a person for confirmations, the recovery sheet's code and a
  re-authentication.
