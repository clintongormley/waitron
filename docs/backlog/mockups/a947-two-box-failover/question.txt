A947 — go-live on two local boxes: four questions, asked one at a time.
Design: docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-21-a947-two-box-failover-design.md (§12).

In short: the main box sells and streams everything it writes to the spare box, about a second
behind, with no internet needed. When the main box dies, a manager switches the venue to the spare
from any till; open tables are still there. When the old main box comes back it hands over the few
sales the spare never saw, and becomes the new spare. Nothing is filed twice and no invoice number
is reused: the spare is switched on under a fresh hour-coded series of its own, chosen at the
moment of the switch (your A679 answer of 2026-10-16), never the main box's.

==========================================================================================
Question 1 of 4 — who switches to the spare box?
Picture: 5-q1-who-switches.png (also 1-main-not-answering.png and 2-take-over-dialog.png)

Example: Friday 21:40, the main box's power supply fails in the middle of service.

- A (recommended) — a manager switches, from any till. After 20 seconds every till shows a red
  strip "El equipo principal no responde"; a manager taps «Cambiar al equipo de reserva», unplugs
  the main box, types the main box's installation code from its recovery sheet, and their password and
  authenticator code. About two minutes without selling (up to an hour if a second switch falls in
  the same clock hour, by your hour-code rule). If the main box is in fact still up (one till's cable is out), the
  dialog refuses and offers a hand-over that loses nothing.
- B — the boxes switch by themselves after about a minute. Faster when the main box really died.
  But two boxes cannot tell "the main box is dead" from "the main box is cut off", so a pulled
  cable or a failed switch between two rooms leaves both boxes selling, each to the tills it can
  reach: fiscally safe, but two sets of open tables. It also needs your W41s-4 answer changed,
  because that take-over asks a person for confirmations, the recovery sheet's code and a
  re-authentication.

Question 2 of 4 — what proves it is a manager at the switch?
Picture: 2-take-over-dialog.png

Example: Friday 21:41, Marta is the manager on shift. Her dashboard password is in her head and
her authenticator app is on her phone in her locker.

- A (recommended) — her password and authenticator code, typed at the till, as switching a box
  over already asks today; if no such manager is on shift, the emergency key from the recovery pack
  instead. It is the proof your W41s answer asked for ("fresh administrator re-authentication"),
  for a step that switches on a fiscal identity that can never be reused.
- B — her till PIN, the one she signs in with all evening. Quicker mid-service, and any shift
  manager has it; but a PIN is short, often seen by colleagues, and it would change what you
  approved for W41s.

Question 3 of 4 — when the old main box comes back, what does it do by itself?
Picture: 4-old-main-returns.png

Example: Saturday 10:05 the repaired main box is plugged back in. It holds three sales from Friday
21:39 that never reached the spare.

- A (recommended) — it hands over the three sales by itself (it has already stopped selling and
  filing, so this is safe), then waits for a manager to tap «Convertir en equipo de reserva»,
  because that wipes it.
- B — it does both by itself: hands over, wipes itself and becomes the spare with nobody touching
  it. Quicker, but a box wipes itself on its own judgement that the hand-over is complete.
- C — it does nothing until a manager starts the hand-over from the dashboard.
