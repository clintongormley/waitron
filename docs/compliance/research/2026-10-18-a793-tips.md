# Asesor research, 2026-10-18: tips, service charges, and who owes what (A793)

**What this is.** The Spanish tax, Social Security, labour and consumer-law research behind the A793
tips design, [2026-10-18-a793-tips-design.md](../../superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-a793-tips-design.md).
It answers seven questions: VAT on a tip; a compulsory service charge; income tax; Social Security
and labour; tips the house keeps; card tips end to end; and 2024–2026 news.

## Summary

- **VAT — CONFIRMS.** A voluntary tip is outside the VAT base, cash or card. The better binding
  authorities are V2182-23 (2023) and a new one, V5110-26 (July 2026), both on tips collected
  electronically and passed on. V3095-17 is cited across the repository as the VAT authority. The
  request put the casino gambling tax as its only direct question; the DGT also answered on
  corporate tax, income tax, VAT and the gaming activities tax because the request discussed them. The ruling does not mention
  cards. V1626-19, which some blogs cite as a tips ruling, is not
  about tips at all.
- **Invoice — ADDS TO Q13(c).** The invoicing regulation lets a simplified invoice carry «cualesquiera
  otras menciones», so a tip may be printed as information; it must not be inside the invoice's
  «Contraprestación total». Nothing requires showing it, and nothing forbids it.
- **Compulsory service charge — NEW.** The EU Court of Justice held in C-404/99 (2001) that a
  compulsory service charge is part of the VAT base. No DGT ruling on a restaurant service charge
  was found. Andalucía's consumer authority says a restaurant may not charge for "servicio" at all.
- **Income tax — ADDS TO L6.** Tips are the employee's income (V2236-13, binding). When the employer
  pays card tips out to staff it must withhold tax on them, with the rest of their pay (1866-03,
  general; RIRPF art. 76.1.3.º). Whether that reaches cash a guest hands straight to a waiter is
  **OPEN**.
- **Social Security — OPEN.** The contribution base counts all pay except a closed list of items,
  and tips are not on the list. The Supreme Court (STS 635/2021, read through a publisher's copy)
  calls tips outside salary, and says their status as a working condition «nada tiene que ver con su
  naturaleza, con su eventual tributación…» (has nothing to do with their nature or possible
  taxation). No Social Security guidance found mentions tips.
- **Sharing — NEW.** A Madrid High Court ruling (2023, read only through a blog) held that card tips
  the business collects are the staff's, the business only holding them, and without a lawful
  agreement with the works council must be shared equally among everyone in the food service. The
  Supreme Court (2021) held that once staff receive tips, the employer can take that away only
  through the formal procedure for changing working conditions.
- **Tips the house keeps — partly CORRECTS.** V3095-17 says tips go into corporate tax only «en la
  medida en que» they are accounting income, on the casinos' own accounting rules. L6 and §11
  present it as a general rule for card tips, which the text does not say. V1808-22 (binding) puts
  tips a company receives for itself into corporate tax. How a restaurant should book card tips it passes on is
  **OPEN**.
- **News.** No Spanish proposal to exempt tips from tax was found.

## 1. VAT on a voluntary tip — CONFIRMS

**Finding.** A tip the guest chooses to give, and need not give, is not payment for the meal, so it
is outside the VAT base whether paid in cash or by card. No ruling read distinguishes cash from card:
the word "tarjeta" (card) appears in none of the rulings in §1, nor in V1808-22, 0905-01 or 1763-99,
and does appear in 1866-03 (§3), so the word search can find it.

**2174-03** (general, 11/12/2003) — the restaurant case:

> «Las cantidades que en concepto de propinas satisfagan con carácter voluntario y unilateral los
> destinatarios de los servicios de restaurante, y que no los sean exigibles por la prestación de
> tales servicios, no constituirán un crédito efectivo a favor de la sociedad consultante ni tampoco
> contraprestación de dichos servicios a efectos del Impuesto sobre el Valor Añadido»

Amounts restaurant guests pay as tips, voluntarily and of their own accord, and which cannot be
demanded for the service, are not a credit in the company's favour nor payment for the service, for
VAT.

**V2182-23** (binding, 25/07/2023, VAT department) — tips collected electronically and passed on:

> «Dicha aplicación permite a los usuarios dejar voluntariamente una propina a los conductores que
> prestan materialmente el servicio, que es transferida por la consultante a los mismos.»

> «las propinas que de manera voluntaria y unilateral satisfagan los clientes a los conductores que
> prestan el servicio de transporte no formarán parte de la base imponible del Impuesto sobre el
> Valor Añadido, no quedando, por tanto, sujetas al mismo»

The company's app lets riders leave a tip, which the company transfers to the drivers; such tips are
not part of the VAT base and are not subject to VAT.

**V5110-26** (binding, 03/07/2026, VAT department) — **new since the 2026-10-09 research**:

> «Los usuarios (seguidores y consumidores de este contenido) pueden realizar donativos a través de
> la plataforma digital, la cual los ingresa en la cuenta del consultante detrayendo una comisión del
> 5% por su gestión.»

> «las propinas que de manera voluntaria y unilateral satisfagan los usuarios al consultante no
> formarán parte de la base imponible del Impuesto sobre el Valor Añadido»

Followers send donations through a platform, which pays them into the creator's account less a 5%
fee; tips paid voluntarily are not part of the VAT base. V5110-26, like V2182-23 and V1808-22, quotes
an earlier ruling on pilgrims' donations (V2604-10). That passage sets the condition §2 turns on:

> «no serán contraprestación de ninguna operación sujeta al Impuesto en la medida en que no se haya
> establecido una cuantía fija para las mismas y quede por tanto su importe a la libre determinación
> de cada peregrino»

They are not payment for anything taxable, in so far as no fixed amount has been set and each giver
decides the amount freely.

**The VAT Act** (art. 78.Uno and 78.Dos.1.º) — the base is the whole consideration, including any
credit arising from the service:

> «La base imponible del impuesto estará constituida por el importe total de la contraprestación de
> las operaciones sujetas al mismo procedente del destinatario o de terceras personas.»

> «cualquier otro crédito efectivo a favor de quien realice la entrega o preste el servicio,
> derivado de la prestación principal o de las accesorias a la misma.»

The Act never uses the word "propina": a word search of the consolidated text (last update
07/10/2026) found none. The tip rule is the DGT's reading of "contraprestación", not a rule in the
Act.

**The invoice — ADDS TO Q13(c).** RD 1619/2012, art. 7.1, the contents of a simplified invoice:

> «Sin perjuicio de los datos o requisitos que puedan resultar obligatorios a otros efectos y de la
> posibilidad de incluir cualesquiera otras menciones, las facturas simplificadas y sus copias
> contendrán los siguientes datos o requisitos:»

> «g) Contraprestación total.»

A simplified invoice may carry any other information besides the required items; one required item
is the total consideration. Our reading: a tip may be printed as information, but since it is not
consideration it must not be inside the total. Nothing read requires a tip to be shown, and nothing
forbids it. Q13(c) said only that no ruling requires or forbids it; this is the regulation's text
behind that.

Neither RD 1007/2023 (the invoicing-software regulation) nor any of the 18 pages of AEAT's
taxpayer Veri*Factu FAQ contains the word "propina". As a control, every one of the 18 pages
contains "factura", so the search reads each page.

**V3095-17 is a weak VAT authority — CORRECTS the repository's citation.** It came from the
department for wealth and transfer taxes, fees and public prices (SG de Impuestos Patrimoniales,
Tasas y Precios Públicos), and the only question put to it was the gambling tax:

> «si bien el escrito de consulta plantea directamente como única cuestión la procedencia o no de que
> las propinas abonadas por los jugadores a los “crupier” de los Casinos de Juego se excluyan de la
> base imponible de la Tasa fiscal que grava los juegos de suerte, envite o azar»

Although the request asked only whether players' tips to croupiers are excluded from the gambling
tax, the DGT went on to comment on the other taxes the request discussed. Its VAT passage repeats
2174-03 and applies it to croupiers:

> «Por lo tanto, las propinas que de manera voluntaria y unilateral satisfagan los clientes de la
> consultante a los crupieres trabajadores de la ésta no formarán parte de la base imponible del
> Impuesto sobre el Valor Añadido.»

It also cites C-404/99 "especially paragraph 16". In the judgment, paragraph 16 records the European
Commission's argument (a tip paid freely is like a passer-by's coin to a street organist), not the
Court's own holding. The casino tips in V3095-17 go into boxes on the gaming tables under the casino
regulation the ruling quotes, and the ruling does not mention cards. So `verifactu-findings.md` §11's
reading, that V3095-17 "is precisely the 'collected by the business' shape … exactly the flow a
card tip follows", goes further than the text. Binding VAT rulings on tips collected
electronically include V2182-23, V5110-26 and V1808-22.

**V1626-19 is not a tips ruling.** Some web pages cite it as one (those pages were not saved). Its
question is:

> «Tributación por el Impuesto sobre el Valor Añadido y tipo impositivo aplicable a la prestación
> del servicio de transporte objeto de consulta.»

How VAT applies, and at what rate, to the transport service described (transport for schools for
pupils with disabilities). The downloaded text does not contain the word "propina". Do not cite it.
Nothing in the repository cites it (grep, 2026-10-18).

**Repository status.** CONFIRMS 2026-10-09 §5.4 bullet 1, Q13(a) and (b) and §11's VAT conclusion.
CORRECTS the choice of V3095-17 as the VAT authority in §11, Q13's banner and L6. ADDS TO Q13(c).

## 2. A compulsory service charge — NEW

**Finding.** A charge the guest must pay, such as a fixed percentage "for service" on every bill, is
part of the price and of the VAT base, even if every cent goes to staff. Consumer law separately
requires the full price to be shown up front, and Andalucía's consumer authority says restaurants
may not charge for service at all.

**CJEU C-404/99**, Commission v France, 29/03/2001. Paragraph 7 defines the charge and sets it apart
from a tip:

> «los recargos obligatorios sobre el precio, incluidos en el precio total facturado al cliente,
> que, por lo general, este último debe abonar en concepto de remuneración del «servicio»»

> «El recargo de servicio se diferencia de las gratificaciones o propinas voluntarias que el cliente
> paga espontánea y directamente a los empleados como muestra de su satisfacción, además del
> mencionado recargo.»

Compulsory surcharges included in the total price billed, which the customer must generally pay as
payment for "service"; these differ from voluntary tips the customer pays spontaneously and directly
to staff, on top of the surcharge.

Paragraphs 42 and 43, the holding:

> «el porcentaje correspondiente a los recargos de servicio debe incluirse en la base imponible del
> IVA de los prestadores de servicios y que en ningún caso cabe admitir que algunos de ellos tengan la
> posibilidad de excluir dichos recargos de su base imponible del IVA»

> «Poco importa, en efecto, para la aplicación del IVA que la clientela haya sido informada de la
> existencia del recargo de servicio y del porcentaje que representa, que las cantidades abonadas por
> este concepto se repartan íntegramente entre los miembros del personal que tienen contacto directo
> con la clientela»

The service percentage must be in the VAT base; no business may leave it out. It does not matter
that customers were told about the charge and its percentage, or that every cent is shared among the
staff who serve them.

**No DGT ruling on a restaurant service charge was found.** PETETE was searched, in both the general
and the binding sets, for "recargo por servicio", "cargo por servicio", "servicio de mesa",
"suplemento de terraza", "cubierto restaurante" and "gratificación camareros". Each search returned
nothing, or only unrelated rulings (customs classifications, business-activity tax headings and
the like). Weaker
than it looks: an empty answer and a failed search look alike. The control, a search for "propina"
through the same script, returned 67 binding rulings, which shows the script works, not that each
phrase was searched as intended. The DGT's own condition in §1 («en la medida en que no se haya
establecido una cuantía fija») points the same way as C-404/99: our reading is that a fixed or
compulsory amount fails it.

**Consumer law** (TRLGDCU). Art. 20.1.c, information in a commercial offer:

> «El precio final completo, incluidos los impuestos, desglosando, en su caso, el importe de los
> incrementos o descuentos que sean de aplicación a la oferta y los gastos adicionales, incluidos los
> potenciales gastos de gestión, que se repercutan al consumidor o usuario.»

Art. 60.2.c, information before the contract:

> «En toda información al consumidor y usuario sobre el precio de los bienes o servicios, incluida
> la publicidad, se informará del precio total, desglosando, en su caso, el importe de los
> incrementos o descuentos que sean de aplicación, de los gastos que se repercutan al consumidor y
> usuario y de los gastos adicionales por servicios accesorios»

Art. 89.5, among the terms that are abusive (unfair, and so void):

> «Los incrementos de precio por servicios accesorios, financiación, aplazamientos, recargos,
> indemnización o penalizaciones que no correspondan a prestaciones adicionales susceptibles de ser
> aceptados o rechazados en cada caso expresados con la debida claridad o separación.»

The full final price, taxes included, must be stated, with any increases and extra charges broken
out; and price increases or surcharges that do not pay for an extra service the customer can accept
or refuse each time, stated clearly and separately, are abusive. Our reading: a compulsory
"servicio" that buys nothing extra is exposed under art. 89.5. That is a reading, not a ruling.

**Andalucía's consumer authority** (Dirección General de Consumo, 29/11/2024):

> «No se puede cobrar ninguna cantidad por cubierto o por servicio porque tanto el servicio como el
> cubierto son inherentes a la prestación que se realiza por el establecimiento.»

> «Dicho aumento de precio no puede establecerse mediante un porcentaje, sino que debe estar indicado
> el precio final que se cobrará por cada servicio en la mesa o terraza.»

No amount may be charged for cover or for service, because both are part of what the restaurant
provides. A higher price for table or terrace service may not be set as a percentage; the final
price for each item must be shown. The note names no regulation for either statement. Other regions
were not read (see Sources).

**History: the "porcentaje de servicio".** The Supreme Court's 2021 ruling (§4) recounts its
2012 decision on the Santa Cruz de Tenerife provincial agreement, art. 31, which by reference to the
old Ordenanza Laboral de Hostelería, art. 52, made employers pay staff, for service, a percentage
of the net amount of the invoices customers paid. It quotes that doctrine:

> «si la empresa carga en la factura al cliente un 15 por 100 destinado al servicio»

> «el empresario debe limitarse a pagar con una mano lo que previamente ha cobrado con la otra con
> destino a idéntica finalidad»

If the business charges the customer 15% for service on the invoice, the employer must simply pay
out with one hand what it collected with the other, for the same purpose. Current agreements treat
the system as being wound down, though not everywhere:

- **Las Palmas 2020–2025, art. 21:** a "complement for the abolition of the service percentage",
  kept only by those who had it:
  > «sólo quienes hubieran consolidado su percepción lo seguirán cobrando hasta la extinción de su
  > contrato laboral.»

  Its art. 5.2 freezes that complement (read; not in the checked quote set).
- **Málaga 2018, third transitional provision:** meetings within three months in "sección sexta"
  establishments
  > «para decidir si los trabajadores optan por la abolición del porcentaje de servicios o seguir con
  > el mismo.»

  A choice between abolishing the percentage and keeping it, not a decision to abolish.
- **Catalonia 2022–2024, art. 35.3:** businesses that paid staff through the service percentage on
  31 December 2007 keep that system unless the staff's representatives expressly agree otherwise
  (read; not in the checked quote set). Neither this nor the other two clauses says whether the
  percentage appears on the customer's bill.

**Repository status.** NEW: no repository file covers a service charge.

## 3. Income tax on tips — ADDS TO L6

**Finding.** Tips are the employee's employment income, to be declared. When the employer pays tips
out to staff, it must withhold income tax on them, worked out together with the rest of their pay.
L6 rested this on V3095-17 (a casino); the restaurant authorities are V2236-13, 1866-03 and the
regulation itself.

**V2236-13** (binding, 08/07/2013, income-tax department), hospitality staff's tips:

> «Las cantidades percibidas en concepto de "propinas" constituyen rendimientos del trabajo para sus
> perceptores, sujetos al Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas y a su sistema de
> retenciones a cuenta»

> «dichas cantidades percibidas en concepto de “propinas” deben consignarse en la declaración del
> Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas físicas, como rendimientos del trabajo.»

Tips are employment income for whoever receives them, subject to income tax and its withholding
system, and must be declared as employment income.

**1866-03** (general, 10/11/2003, income-tax department) — **new to the repository**, and the
closest to Waitron's case: card tips a hospitality business collects and shares out.

> «Al utilizarse como medio de pago la tarjeta de crédito o débito, cargándose a petición del
> cliente una cantidad adicional correspondiente a propina, cantidad que posteriormente la empresa
> reparte entre los empleados, se pregunta sobre su sometimiento a retención.»

> «Una vez establecida la obligación de retener sobre las propinas»

> «tal cálculo se realizará (junto con las restantes retribuciones del empleado) de acuerdo con el
> procedimiento general»

The question: when a guest pays by credit or debit card and asks for a tip to be added, which the
company later shares among employees, must tax be withheld? Answer: there is a duty to withhold, and
the withholding is worked out together with the employee's other pay under the general procedure.
It applied the 1999 regulation; the current art. 76.1.3.º, quoted next, has the same words.

**RIRPF art. 76.1** (RD 439/2007, last update 07/10/2026). The general rule, then the exception that
names tips:

> «No se considerará que una persona o entidad satisface rentas cuando se limite a efectuar una
> simple mediación de pago.»

> «3.º Cuando satisfagan a su personal cantidades desembolsadas por terceros en concepto de propina,
> retribución por el servicio u otros similares.»

Someone who only passes a payment on is not treated as paying income, except (among other cases)
when they pay their own staff amounts third parties gave as a tip or for service. Art. 76 is the only
place in the downloaded RIRPF where the word "propina" appears.

**Income Tax Act art. 17.1**, the definition the rulings rely on:

> «Se considerarán rendimientos íntegros del trabajo todas las contraprestaciones o utilidades,
> cualquiera que sea su denominación o naturaleza, dinerarias o en especie, que deriven, directa o
> indirectamente, del trabajo personal o de la relación laboral o estatutaria y no tengan el
> carácter de rendimientos de actividades económicas.»

Employment income is everything, whatever it is called, that comes directly or indirectly from
work. The Act never uses the word "propina" (word search, last update 07/10/2026), so it has no
exemption for tips.

**1763-99** (general, 04/10/1999) — a waiter who

> «recibe de los clientes la llamada "propina" que se reparte entre los trabajadores según
> costumbre.»

and the DGT adds

> «que el concepto de "propinas" tiene la consideración de retribución de carácter variable.»

Tips shared among staff by custom count as variable pay when the withholding rate is worked out.

**Cash handed straight to the waiter — OPEN.** 2174-03 (§5) says, without separating cash from
card:

> «Por tanto, en aplicación del precepto transcrito, la entidad consultante deberá practicar la
> retención correspondiente por las propinas que reciban sus trabajadores.»

The restaurant must withhold on the tips its workers receive. But art. 76.1.3.º is about amounts the
employer «satisfagan a su personal» (pays to its staff). Our reading: a note the guest hands
straight to the waiter is not paid by the employer, so art. 76 may not reach it, and the waiter
declares it. No source settles which reading is right. That is L6(d).

Cash left in the till and shared out at the close of day is a third case. Our reading is that the
employer then pays it to its staff, in art. 76.1.3.º's words; no source addresses it. That is
L6(g).

**V3095-17** says the same for croupiers:

> «En consecuencia, de acuerdo con lo expuesto las propinas estarán sujetas a retención»

So L6's statement that, for employees, the tips are employment income subject to withholding is in
V3095-17's text. What changes is the authority: 1866-03 and art. 76.1.3.º are on point for a
restaurant's card tips.

**Repository status.** ADDS TO L6 (new authorities, and the cash case split out as open).

## 4. Social Security and labour — contribution base OPEN

**Finding.** No source settles whether tips paid through payroll count towards Social Security
contributions. The law counts all pay except a closed list, and tips are not on it. The Supreme Court
(STS 635/2021, read through a publisher's copy) calls tips outside salary, and says their status as
a working condition «nada tiene que ver con su naturaleza, con su eventual tributación…» (has
nothing to do with their nature or possible taxation). Labour law is
clearer: once staff receive tips, the employer may not simply stop it, and a Madrid court has said
how card tips must be shared when nothing has been agreed.

**LGSS art. 147** (last update 03/10/2026). The base (147.1):

> «estará constituida por la remuneración total, cualquiera que sea su forma o denominación, tanto
> en metálico como en especie, que con carácter mensual tenga derecho a percibir el trabajador o
> asimilado, o la que efectivamente perciba de ser esta superior, por razón del trabajo que realice
> por cuenta ajena.»

The exclusions (147.2) are a closed list:

> «2. Únicamente no se computarán en la base de cotización los siguientes conceptos:»

Every pay item must be reported (147.3):

> «Los empresarios deberán comunicar a la Tesorería General de la Seguridad Social en cada período
> de liquidación el importe de todos los conceptos retributivos abonados a sus trabajadores, con
> independencia de su inclusión o no en la base de cotización»

The base is the worker's total pay "by reason of the work", in any form; "only" the listed items
are left out; and the employer must report every pay item to the TGSS, whether or not it counts. The
word "propina" appears nowhere in the LGSS, nor in the contribution regulation RD 2064/1995 (word
searches of both downloaded texts).

**Workers' Statute art. 26.1**, what salary is:

> «Se considerará salario la totalidad de las percepciones económicas de los trabajadores, en dinero
> o en especie, por la prestación profesional de los servicios laborales por cuenta ajena»

The Statute does not use the word "propina" either.

**TS 635/2021** (Supreme Court, labour chamber, 17/06/2021, appeal 180/2019; Serunión, which runs
five cafeterías in Asturias, most in hospitals). **Read through laboral-social.com's copy, not the
court's database.** One sentence is also on the court's own press note (marked below). The court
restates its doctrine:

> «En suma, las propinas se excluyen del concepto de salario, al ser percibidas por el trabajador con
> ocasión de su trabajo, por no proceder del ingreso del patrimonio empresarial sino de un tercero.»

> «Salvo en el especial caso de los Casinos, las propinas son ajenas al salario garantizado y poseen
> naturaleza extra salarial.»

Tips are not salary: the worker receives them on the occasion of the work, from a third party, not
from the business's assets. Except in casinos, they are outside guaranteed pay. (The bullet before
the first quote limits this to tips "in so far as they exceed the pay guaranteed by the company";
read, not in the checked quote set.) The court then separates the working condition from the tips'
nature and tax:

> «Este carácter laboral del derecho a percibir propinas nada tiene que ver con su naturaleza, con su
> eventual tributación o con los problemas que suscita el que se excluya de su disfrute a quienes
> carecen de fijeza.»

The chance to receive tips is a working condition, and that has nothing to do with their nature or
their possible taxation. On the employer's power:

> «la empresa, ejerciendo sus poderes directivos, tiene en su mano la posibilidad de permitir su
> disfrute o de vedarlo»

> «los convenios colectivos han podido adoptar decisiones sobre su distribución.»

> «Desde luego, consideramos que la empresa puede acordar válidamente la prohibición de que su
> plantilla sea gratificada por la clientela, por así derivar de su poder de organización y
> dirección (arts. 1.1 y 20.1 ET). Ahora bien, cuando esa posibilidad de ganancia económica y
> recompensa moral preexiste ha de seguir el procedimiento fijado al efecto por el artículo 41 ET»

The employer may allow or forbid tips, and collective agreements may decide how they are shared. A
business may forbid staff from accepting tips, but where staff already had that chance, removing it
must follow the Statute's art. 41 procedure for a substantial change to working conditions.
Confirmed word for word on the **CGPJ press note** (07/07/2021):

> «ni el carácter extrasalarial de la propina ni su dependencia de la voluntad de terceras personas
> impiden que la posibilidad de recibir esas liberalidades de la clientela se considere como una
> verdadera condición de trabajo»

The press note also says the business had shared tips only among permanent staff, which the court
held contrary to Spanish and EU rules, extending the right to temporary staff:

> «hasta ahora las propinas se repartían solo entre los trabajadores fijos pero no entre los
> temporales»

One of the five judges dissented: the employer could decide without the art. 41 procedure (press
note; read, not in the checked quote set).

**Social Security guidance.** Two documents were read: a TGSS Gestión Recaudatoria note on the 2013
change to the contribution base, which says every salary item the article does not expressly exclude
must be included, and a Gómez-Acebo & Pombo paper on the same change. Neither contains the word
"propina". A 2026 trade article (InfoHoreca) says tips the business manages must go through payroll
with withholding and, if habitual, Social Security contributions; it gives no source for the
contribution point, and nothing here relies on it.

Our reading of the gap: the base counts total pay "by reason of the work", and its list of
exclusions is closed and silent on tips, which points to inclusion; the Supreme Court calls tips
non-salary, and says their status as a working condition «nada tiene que ver con su naturaleza, con
su eventual tributación…» (has nothing to do with their nature or possible taxation). Which way the TGSS reads a tip paid through
payroll is not in any source found. That is L6(c), and L6(f) asks how to report it under 147.3.

**TSJ Madrid, 31/05/2023 (Hotel Villamagna) — read only through Eduardo Rojo's blog
(05/07/2023); the ruling's CENDOJ number was not found.** About 85 workers in the hotel's food
department (café, restaurant, room service and kitchen, per the blog). Card tips had been shared only
among waiters. The court, as quoted by the blog:

> «las propinas que la empresa recauda a través del pago telemático, en aplicación de esta norma y, a
> falta de un acuerdo consensuado, ponderado, razonable y lícito por parte del comité de empresa, han
> de ser repartidas de forma igualitaria a todo el personal que interviene en el servicio de
> restauración»

> «de tal manera que tiene en su poder una determinada cantidad de la que es mera tenedora al ser sus
> destinatarios los trabajadores del restaurante»

Card tips the business collects must, without an agreed, balanced, reasonable and lawful agreement
from the works council, be shared equally among all staff in the food service; the business merely
holds money that belongs to the restaurant's workers. «esta norma» is the Civil Code's art. 637,
read on the BOE:

> «Cuando la donación hubiere sido hecha a varias personas conjuntamente, se entenderá por partes
> iguales; y no se dará entre ellas el derecho de acrecer, si el donante no hubiese dispuesto otra
> cosa.»

A gift made to several people jointly is taken to be in equal shares unless the giver said
otherwise. The blog says the court followed a TSJ País Vasco ruling of 18/07/2017, whose official summary,
as the blog gives it, names floor and kitchen staff. The business's own position, from the first-instance
facts quoted by the blog, shows where cash sits:

> «no quiere ni puede intervenir en la fórmula de reparto, ni procederá a la modificación de las
> condiciones departamentales de núcleos de negocio nunca afectados por estas supuestas
> discrepancias, ni por ende entrar a repartir propinas en efectivo que no pasan por el control de la
> empresa»

It would not share out cash tips that never pass through its hands.

**Collective agreements.** None of the sampled agreements (see Sources) contains the word "propina":
the national ALEH VI and its 2026 amendment, and the Las Palmas, Málaga, Ávila and Catalonia
agreements. A sample of four, not a survey of Spain's provincial agreements.

**Repository status.** NEW: no repository file covers Social Security or sharing. L6 asked only about
booking and withholding.

## 5. Tips the house keeps, and how it books them — partly CORRECTS

**Finding.** A restaurant that passes tips on is a go-between: the DGT said in 2003 that such tips
are neither its income nor its expense. Tips a company receives for itself are its income for
corporate tax (V1808-22), and remain outside VAT while voluntary. V3095-17's corporate-tax line, which
L6 and §11 read as covering card tips, is conditional and rests on casino accounting rules. How a
restaurant should book card tips it passes on is **OPEN**.

**2174-03** (general, 2003, under the corporate tax law then in force, Ley 43/1995, since repealed):

> «la entidad recibe las propinas como mero intermediario entre sus clientes y sus camareros, y no
> como retribución por la prestación de un servicio o la entrega de un bien»

> «las cantidades recibidas como propinas no constituyen ingreso fiscal, puesto que la entidad
> consultante actúa como intermediario en la remuneración a terceros. En consecuencia y por la misma
> razón, tampoco su reparto entre el personal tendrá la consideración de gasto fiscal.»

The restaurant receives tips as a mere intermediary between guests and waiters, not as payment for
anything, so they are not taxable income and sharing them out is not a deductible expense. The same
ruling still requires withholding (§3).

**V3095-17** (binding, 2017, casino):

> «en la medida en que las propinas recibidas por la entidad consultante tenga la consideración de
> ingreso contable, el importe de las mismas deberá integrarse en la base imponible del Impuesto
> sobre Sociedades»

In so far as the tips the company receives count as accounting income, they go into its
corporate-tax base. The accounting rule the ruling cites for that is the casinos' own:

> «La Orden de 23 de junio de 1978 por la que se aprueban las normas de Adaptación del Plan General
> de Contabilidad para los Casinos de Juego»

That casino accounting plan, the ruling says, has an income account that points to the casino
regulation's article on tips, which the ruling quotes in full: tips go into boxes on the tables and
form a pool from which casino salaries and contributions must be paid (read; not in the checked quote
set). On the expense side:

> «No obstante, en el caso de que la remuneración de los empleados con cargo al tronco de propinas no
> se derive del contrato celebrado entre el trabajador y la consultante, dicho gasto tendría la
> consideración de liberalidad»

If paying staff from the tips pool does not arise from their employment contracts, the expense is a
gift, which the ruling goes on to say is not deductible.

**The correction.** L6 says V3095-17's "own reasoning is that when the house collects tips into a
*tronco* and redistributes them, they are an *ingreso contable* that integrates into the base of
the Impuesto sobre Sociedades". The text makes that conditional («en la medida en que»), and the
condition is met by a casino accounting rule that a restaurant does not follow. §11 goes further, to
"a tip that flows through Waitron's card terminal is business income", which no ruling read says.
The 2026-10-09 research called the two rulings a conflict; with the condition read, V3095-17 does not
state a general rule a restaurant could set against 2174-03.

**V1808-22** (binding, 29/07/2022, corporate-tax department) — a website's tip button, the tips
going to the company itself:

> «los ingresos derivados de las “propinas” obtenidas por la consultante deberán integrarse en la
> base imponible del Impuesto sobre Sociedades»

> «no determinarán la realización de operaciones sujetas al impuesto en la medida en que no
> constituyan la remuneración de entregas de bienes o prestaciones de servicios realizados por la
> consultante a favor de las personas o entidades que las satisfacen»

Tips the company itself obtains go into its corporate-tax base; for VAT they are not taxable, in so
far as they do not pay for something the company supplies to the giver. It was also asked:

> «3 ¿Debería la empresa emitir factura o algún tipo de ticket o recibo por cada uno de estos
> ingresos, o sería suficiente con la documentación generada y entregada al "donante" por la
> plataforma de pago?»

whether an invoice or receipt is needed for each tip. In the downloaded text the word "factura"
appears only in the questions, so the answer does not address it. The ruling also declines the
accounting question as outside the DGT's remit (read; not in the checked quote set).

**0905-01** (general, 14/05/2001, VAT department) — a company whose only income is "propinas" of
100 pesetas that car-park users give voluntarily:

> «constituyen una prestación de servicios de carácter empresarial, sujeta y no exenta del Impuesto
> sobre el Valor Añadido»

Its car-park service is a business service subject to VAT. The ruling does not explain why the
payments count; our reading is that where a "tip" is really the price of the service, VAT applies.

**Audiencia Nacional, 2001 — not read.** Garrigues (18/09/2018) cites a 2001 Audiencia Nacional
ruling holding that tips a restaurant books and passes on to staff are neither taxable income nor a
deductible expense, the restaurant being a mere intermediary; the key, it says, was that management
gave no instruction on how to share them. The ruling itself was not found or read.

**Booking — OPEN.** No ruling of the ICAC (Spain's accounting standards institute) on tips was found.
Whether card tips are income and expense, or a debt to staff, is the accountant's question (Q49).

**Repository status.** Partly CORRECTS L6, §11 and Q13's banner (the corporate-tax claim). ADDS
V1808-22's corporate-tax line and 0905-01.

## 6. Card tips, end to end

Putting §§1–5 together for a tip a guest adds to a card payment, which the venue passes to staff:

1. **VAT:** outside the base, and outside the invoice total (§1: 2174-03, V2182-23, V5110-26).
2. **Income tax:** the employer withholds when it pays the tip out, worked out with the rest of the
   employee's pay (§3: 1866-03, RIRPF art. 76.1.3.º).
3. **Sharing:** without a lawful agreement with the works council, equally among all food-service
   staff (§4: TSJ Madrid 2023, through a blog only). Once staff receive tips, stopping it needs the
   art. 41 procedure (§4: TS 635/2021).
4. **Social Security:** **OPEN** (§4).
5. **Booking:** income and expense, or a debt to staff — **OPEN** (§5).
