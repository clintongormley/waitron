# Adviser research, 2026-11-06: what "online access" really requires (T4)

Follows the owner's request of 6 November to find out what is really required, given that many venues
will not have their data on the internet. It replaces the "no source says" of Q48g in the
[tips and declaration report](2026-11-06-adviser-tips-and-declaration.md).
This is research, not legal advice.

## The short answer

**The law asks for the tax office to be able to reach the documents when it asks. It does not ask for
the data to be on the internet.** «En línea» here means a live look at the system, and the ministerial
order that develops the rule covers local storage by name.

What a venue owes, on our reading:

- Only when the tax office asks: article 23 applies to a body «que esté realizando una actuación de
  comprobación», and article 21.2 says «ante cualquier solicitud de esta y sin demora injustificada».
- Four abilities over every invoice: see it in full, search it, copy or download it in its original
  format, and print it.
- A network link only «en caso necesario», where the data sits on a remote database.
- No permanent connection, public address or prior registration appears in any text read.

**How the reading is built.** Texts 1, 4 and 7 use the word «en línea» and stop there. Text 8 is the only
one that says what it means, and it says so for local storage. Text 10 is the only one that says when a
network link is needed. Texts 15, 16 and 18 show the system assumes inspectors look at the system in
person and take copies away.

## What Veri\*Factu changes

Each billing record already reaches AEAT, so AEAT holds the number, date, totals and VAT breakdown
online for those invoices without anyone doing anything. AEAT's FAQ says these systems need not keep
the records «ya que se han remitido a la Agencia Tributaria». That does not cover invoice copies: «No se
debe confundir la conservación de las facturas exigida por el ROF con la conservación de los RF». The
record lacks the invoice lines, so the copies are still what an inspector would ask the venue for.

## The options for a venue

| Route | Who it suits | How | What to watch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Through Waitron Cloud | Cloud customers | The tax office reaches the venue's data through Waitron's servers (the owner's plan, not built) | If Cloud keeps a copy, Waitron is a third party under art. 19.3, and the venue stays responsible |
| At the venue | Every venue; the default | A read-only sign-in on the box's own network: view, search, copy, print. Texts 8, 15 and 18 | Needs the screen below. Someone in charge must be able to open it |
| A copy of the period | Every venue, for a written request | Export the period in original formats and hand it over on a USB drive or by download. Texts 10, 11 and 16 | Text 16 is about billing records; for invoice copies this rests on text 11. T4 asks for confirmation |
| A temporary link | A venue with its own storage account | Put the export in the account and share a link during the check | The provider becomes a data processor. Whether a short-lived copy abroad is "keeping abroad" under art. 22.2 is unsettled, so a Spanish region avoids the point |
| A third party keeps it | A venue whose gestoría offers this | Regular exports to the gestoría, under art. 19.3 | The venue stays responsible. Art. 19.4 adds a notice for a keeper outside the EU and similar countries |
| Paper | Rarely | Art. 20.1 allows it, and arts. 21.2 and 23 then do not apply | Thermal paper fades and tickets pile up. An emailed invoice stays electronic in its original format (art. 21.1), though V2187-14 allows paper in some cases |

**Recommendation.** Build one read-only page for the tax office and one period export, on the box. Those
two meet the default routes for every venue, and Cloud can show the same page through its own servers,
so a venue without Cloud gets the same capability. The temporary link, third-party and paper routes
need nothing from Waitron beyond the export.

## What Waitron lacks today

Found by reading the code, not by running it.

- The dashboard has no invoice list. `getSalesPeriod` and the Sales screen show totals.
- The till's `lookUpInvoices` finds only full invoices with a named customer, at most 20
  (`INVOICE_LOOKUP_LIMIT`), so a simplified ticket cannot be found that way.
- No export of invoice copies for a period exists. A664's archive and reader are proposed, not built.
- There is no read-only sign-in for an inspector, so text 17 has no answer yet.

The search must cover «cualquiera de los datos que deban reflejarse en los libros registro» (text 8).
RIVA art. 63.3 lists these as the number and series, issue date, operation date if different, customer's
name and tax number, taxable base, VAT rate and amount.

## Limits

- No source deals with a restaurant's box with no public address. This is a reading of general texts.
- Order 962/2007 was written for the earlier regulation and survives only where it does not contradict
  RD 1619/2012. Article 21.2 is unchanged, so we see no contradiction. The rulings say this in passing
  while discussing digitisation, and nobody has ruled on article 5.3 itself. A court has not been read.
- DGT 0289-04 and 2021-03 are old, non-binding and predate today's law. Of the two, only 2021-03 is about
  tickets.
- How inspectors act in practice is unknown. The text says what they may demand, not what they usually do.
- Search covered the DGT database for «acceso en línea» and «carga remota»: 69 documents, many on other
  subjects. Rulings were read at the passages quoted, not all in full. As in the earlier reports, rulings
  after 30 September 2026 were not in the database.
- BOE's page for the order lists no repeal among its later references, and it holds the text as published
  in 2007, not a consolidated one.
- A text search of the Commission's explanatory notes on invoicing (2011) finds neither "online" nor
  "on-line", so they do not help.
