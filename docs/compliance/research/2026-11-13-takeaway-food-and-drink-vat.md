# Takeaway and delivery food and drink: which VAT rate

Researched on 13 November 2026 for adviser question
[T17](../2026-11-06-adviser-questions.md#the-questions-that-need-judgement). This is research, not legal
advice.

## What the sources say

Food and drink served to guests at the venue by its own staff is a restaurant service, taxed at 10%
as a whole, drinks included. When the venue only hands food or drink over, as with takeaway or
delivery, it supplies goods instead. Goods take each item's own rate: food 10%, but alcoholic drinks
and sugared soft drinks, juices and fizzy drinks are excluded from the 10% list, so they take the
general 21%.

The DGT ruling the owner cited, V2949-16, is about catering in a customer's home. It never says «para
llevar», and it mentions home delivery only when quoting the EU Court of Justice on catering firms.
Its general paragraph sets the test: eaten on the premises and served by the venue's staff is a
service; «en otro caso», a supply of goods. On our reading, takeaway and delivery fall in that other
case, and the ruling's conclusion says the same of ready-made food handed over with no real service. No source found says how a single price for a takeaway set
menu is split between rates; that is what T17 asks.

The ruling quotes article 91.Uno.1.1.º as it stood in 2016, with the alcohol exclusion only. The
sugared-drink exclusion b) is in the current text above.
