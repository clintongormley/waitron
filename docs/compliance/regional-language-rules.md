# Language rules for restaurants in Spain, by region

Researched 2026-09-30. This is research only; no code was changed.

**How it was done.** Every quote below was taken from the source's own text. I downloaded each page or
PDF with `curl`, stripped the markup (or ran `pdftotext` on PDFs), and searched it for the article. I
did not use a summary tool for quotes. The researcher kept the raw copies outside the repository; they are not committed. Where the official
regional portal only loads its text through JavaScript (the Catalan legal portal, the Valencian
bulletin's record pages), I used the **BOE consolidated text** of the same law. BOE, the Spanish state
gazette, republishes regional laws in Spanish, with a note on each amended article. That Spanish text
is an official publication, but it is not the original-language version. Each row says which version
it quotes.

**"Consolidated"** means the current text with every later amendment applied. BOE shows the date of the
last amendment it has applied, and I give that date where it matters. A BOE consolidation can lag
behind the law. Catalonia's is one example: see the note under "Enforcement" in the Catalonia table.

## Summary in plain English

1. **Spain as a whole does not care what language a till receipt or invoice is in.** The invoicing
   regulation says invoices "may be issued in any language". The tax office may ask for a translation
   into Spanish, or another official language of Spain, only for invoices written in a language that
   is *not* official in Spain. Veri\*Factu adds no general language rule. It does fix three short texts
   that it gives only in Spanish: the label «QR tributario:» above the QR code, and the phrase
   «Factura verificable en la sede electrónica de la AEAT». The regulation also lets you print the
   shorter «VERI\*FACTU» instead of that phrase, and those words look the same in every language. I
   found no provision that allows those texts to be translated.

2. **Catalonia is the only region that clearly requires a restaurant's own consumer documents to be in
   the regional language.**
   - **Menus and fixed information:** fixed signs, fixed notices and "documents offering services" must
     be *at least* in Catalan. Catalonia's consumer agency says in so many words that a restaurant's
     menu (*carta*) must be available at least in Catalan.
   - **Receipts and invoices:** customers have a statutory *right to receive* invoices in Catalan. The
     agency reads that as meaning invoices and till receipts must be at least in Catalan.
   - **Spanish:** Spanish is never forbidden. Spain's Constitutional Court (ruling 88/2017) upheld the
     Catalan rule only on the reading that customers can also get the same documents in Spanish.
   - **Who it applies to:** every business; there is no size threshold.

3. **Valencian Community:** menus and price lists must be available in **both** official languages
   (Valencian and Spanish), plus at least one foreign language, preferably English. That applies on
   paper and in digital form, in every bar and restaurant open to the public (Decree 36/2023). I found
   no rule on the language of receipts or invoices.

4. **Galicia:** a restaurant's or cafeteria's list of services and prices must be in Galician **and**
   Spanish. The menu must be in Galician and Spanish, and 3-, 4- and 5-fork restaurants need two
   foreign languages as well (Decree 108/2006, amended by Decree 8/2007). I found no language rule for
   invoices. Galicia's consumer law lets a business use either official language.

5. **Balearic Islands:** the rule is the opposite of Catalonia's. Menus and price lists must be in *at
   least one* of the two official languages (Catalan or Spanish), so Spanish alone is lawful. Consumer
   information generally follows the same rule. The one fixed multilingual text is the notice that
   complaint forms are available, which must be in Catalan, Spanish, French, English and German. I
   found no language rule for receipts.

6. **Basque Country:** the rules depend on the size of the business.
   - **Who is covered:** since 2023, the binding duties in Law 4/2023 cover only providers of basic
     services (utilities, transport, finance and the like) and "large commercial establishments". For
     every other private business, the government is only told to *promote* Basque. An ordinary
     restaurant is not in either named group; that is my reading, and neither the law nor official
     guidance says so for restaurants specifically.
   - **What covered businesses must do:** give invoices and similar documents in Basque and Spanish
     (or bilingual), unless the customer picks one language.
   - **Is the 2008 decree still in force?** The 2008 decree (123/2008) reached further: it covered
     establishments belonging to large companies (more than 250 staff, plus a turnover or balance-sheet
     test). Law 4/2023 repealed that decree only "in so far as it conflicts" with the new law. Whether
     its wider list of businesses still applies is **not established**.
   - **Sanctions:** I found no offence in the 2023 law for breaking the language rules. Its catch-all
     offence expressly leaves them out.

7. **Navarre:** I found **no** language obligation for private businesses: none in the consumer
   statute, none in the Basque-language law and none in the restaurant regulation.

8. **Spain-wide consumer law (possibly relevant):**
   - Information given to a consumer before a contract must be "at least in Spanish". If either side
     asks, it must also be given in another official language of the place.
   - Mandatory labelling and presentation of goods and services must be at least in Spanish.
   - Whether a restaurant menu counts as "information given before a contract" is my inference, not
     something a source states.

9. **Large companies only (from 2026):** Law 10/2025 on customer service requires companies to accept
   complaints and give customer service in a region's official language. It covers companies with at
   least 250 staff, or above a turnover or balance-sheet figure, and utility-type services. It is about
   customer-service channels, not receipts.

10. **Descriptions of individual items.** None of the laws separates the fixed printed wording from
    what is sold. They speak of "the invoice", "the menu" or "the price list" as a whole. Catalonia's
    agency says the *carta* (the list of dishes) must be at least in Catalan, which in practice covers
    the dish names. Whether a proper name, such as a brand or a dish's traditional name, must be
    translated is **not established**.

## Where two sources seem to contradict

- **Catalonia: "right to receive" versus "must be at least in Catalan".** The two describe different
  things.
  - The Consumer Code's art. 128-1 gives the *customer* a right to receive invoices in Catalan.
  - Llei 1/1998 art. 32.3 *requires* fixed information and "documents offering services" to be in
    Catalan, whether or not anyone asks.
  - The consumer agency combines both into "must be at least in Catalan" for menus *and* invoices.
    For invoices, that is the agency's reading, not the statute's wording.
- **Catalonia: invoices "at least in Catalan" for public-service companies versus consumers'
  rights.** Llei 1/1998 arts. 30–31 (invoices "and other *documents de tràfic*" at least in Catalan,
  with Spanish on request) cover *public* companies and *public-service* companies. A private
  restaurant falls under the Consumer Code instead.
- **Balearic Islands versus Catalonia.** Both regions have Catalan as an official language, but the
  Balearic rules require only *one* of the two official languages, where Catalonia requires Catalan.
- **Basque Country: the 2008 decree versus the 2023 law.** The decree put duties on establishments
  of large companies. The 2023 law names only basic-service providers and large commercial
  establishments, and repealed the decree only "in so far as it conflicts". Kontsumobide's guidance
  page still describes the decree, and gives a threshold (500 staff) that differs from the decree's
  text (250).
- **Spain-wide Law 10/2025 versus the regional laws.** Law 10/2025 is about customer-service
  channels at large companies and utility-type services. It is not about receipts or menus.
